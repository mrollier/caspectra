\begin{abstract}
Damage-response statistics of a cellular automaton (CA)---whether, how far and
how densely the difference between two runs that differ in one cell
spreads---are defined through \emph{twin} simulations, so estimating them from a
single observed space--time diagram is a natural amortization task for learned
models. We report a benchmark, with decision rules committed in advance, over
the $88$ elementary-CA orbits and $800$ uniformly sampled two-state radius-two
rules (a $2^{32}$-rule space), under one explicit observation model:
\emph{fully observed, noiseless, deterministic, synchronous, binary} diagrams of
\emph{known neighbourhood radius}. Under these matched assumptions a single
$127$-cell diagram identifies the entire local rule table for $100\%$
(elementary) and $97.5\%$ (radius-two) of held-out rules, and the transparent
system-identification estimator---reconstruct the rule, then re-run the
perturbation experiment---predicts all four damage-response statistics at the
accuracy of an \emph{independent Monte-Carlo replicate} of the target itself
(e.g.\ damage survival $0.985$ vs.\ a replicate-agreement benchmark of $0.987$
on the elementary space). We distinguish this simulation-limited benchmark
($2\,\mathrm{ICC}-1$ under additive noise) from the latent-reliability ceiling
(ICC) with which it is easily conflated, and verify against a $16\times$-larger
reference simulation that the estimator also attains the latter. Two
\emph{direct} single-diagram estimators---a regressor on five cheap statistics
and a deliberately constrained convolutional amortiser---fall far short of the
benchmark on the least texture-like target and show no stable ordering between
themselves; a cross-fitted stacking test shows the network carries real
information beyond the five statistics for damage survival (incremental \rtwo{}
$+0.32$). An identifiability phase diagram then maps where reading the rule
stops working: under observation noise above ${\sim}2\%$, heavy masking, or
extreme initial-condition densities, exact reconstruction collapses and the
frozen network degrades more gracefully---the regime in which direct
amortization has a rationale. Two descriptive by-products (an interpretable
phenotype map for non-uniform CA and a damage-signature landscape of the
radius-two space) are validated against independent ground truth with
conservative language. The general lesson for machine-learning benchmarks on
deterministic dynamical systems is structural: when the observation encodes the
generator, system identification is the baseline to beat.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Cellular automata (CA) are the canonical minimal model of emergent computation:
a single local update rule, iterated in parallel, produces the full zoo of
behaviours from frozen order to chaos to the localized interacting ``particles''
of complex rules~\cite{wolfram2002,langton1990}. Classifying that behaviour is a
long-standing and subtle problem, because the field distinguishes the
\emph{genotype}---the local update rule---from the \emph{phenotype}---the
mesoscopic structure of the space--time diagram it generates. A method intended
to read behaviour off the diagram must be prevented from instead reading the
\emph{rule} off the diagram, which is almost always possible: a diagram
contains, in its local neighbourhoods, the entire rule table, and a
convolutional network recovers the elementary rule with $99.9\%$ accuracy unless
architecturally impeded~\cite{rollier2024cnn}. This is a textbook instance of
\emph{shortcut learning}~\cite{geirhos2020shortcut}.

Formalized Wolfram-style class membership is undecidable~\cite{culikyu1988}, and
the class observed in practice depends on the initial-condition
measure~\cite{gilman1987}, the lattice, and the horizon. The defensible object
of study is therefore not ``the class of a rule'' but a \emph{protocol
tuple}---(rule orbit, IC measure, lattice, horizon)---characterized by
finite-horizon, perturbation-defined statistics. The best-posed of these is
\emph{damage spreading}: evolve two copies of a configuration that differ in a
single cell and measure how the difference grows~\cite{vispoel2024damage,
vispoel2022progress}. We call the resulting quantities \emph{damage-response
statistics}, not invariants: they are finite-size, finite-horizon and
measure-dependent by construction.

Because damage-response statistics require \emph{two} runs of the simulator,
they are not a function of any single observed diagram, which makes them a
natural target for \emph{amortization}: train an encoder to predict, from one
diagram, a quantity that ordinarily requires a perturbation experiment. But
under the observation model in which such benchmarks are usually posed---the
diagram is complete, noiseless, binary, generated by a deterministic synchronous
CA of known radius---there is a transparent alternative: \emph{infer the rule
from the diagram and simulate the statistic}. This is matched-model system
identification, and this paper measures what it does to the benchmark. Three
results organize everything that follows.

\emph{First}, the identification problem is essentially solved by a single
diagram: the rule table is recovered exactly for $100\%$ of elementary and
$97.5\%$ of sampled radius-two held-out rules, and reconstruct-and-simulate then
predicts the damage response at the accuracy of an independent Monte-Carlo
replicate of the target itself---the correct ``simulation-limited'' benchmark,
which we carefully separate from the latent-reliability ceiling
(Sec.~\ref{sec:reliability}). \emph{Second}, the two direct estimators we
trained---a five-statistic regressor and a convolutional amortiser built to
resist first-layer rule reading---fall well short of that benchmark exactly
where texture is least informative (damage survival), and neither dominates the
other; the network does, however, carry survival information the five
statistics miss, a real and quantified complementarity. \emph{Third}, the
mechanistic route is brittle outside its assumptions: an identifiability phase
diagram (Sec.~\ref{sec:identifiability}) shows exact reconstruction collapsing
under modest observation noise, heavy masking, or extreme initial-condition
density, with the frozen network degrading more gracefully---delineating,
rather than asserting away, the regime where learned amortization can earn its
keep.

We committed decision rules, margins, and rule panels to a timestamped
repository before the corresponding measurements; Appendix~\ref{app:repro}
labels each analysis as \emph{registered}, \emph{post-hoc confirmatory}, or
\emph{exploratory}, and the repository is released with the article.

\section{Benchmark}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Rule spaces and the observation model}
We use two rule spaces. \emph{Elementary CA} (ECA): two states, radius one,
$2^{2^3}=256$ rules, of which $88$ are distinct representatives under the
reflection and complementation symmetries a behavioural method should respect.
\emph{Radius-two CA}: two states, a five-cell neighbourhood, a $32$-entry table
and $2^{32}\approx4\times10^{9}$ rules; ECA embeds exactly (a continuity
control). The radius-two space is sampled uniformly ($800$ canonical rules after
orbit deduplication).

Every quantity is indexed by a fixed \emph{observation protocol}: a
Bernoulli$(1/2)$ initial condition on a ring of $127$ cells (non-power-of-two,
so additive rules do not collapse to homogeneity), evolved from $t=0$; the
damage horizon is $\lfloor w/(2r)\rfloor-1$ steps so a perturbation cone cannot
wrap. The observation model deliberately matches the generator: diagrams are
\emph{complete, noiseless, binary, synchronous in the observed time direction,
spatially uniform in the rule, and of known radius}. These assumptions are load
bearing---they are what make the rule readable---and
Sec.~\ref{sec:identifiability} measures what happens when each is relaxed.

\subsection{Damage-response statistics}
The targets are four scalars per rule, estimated from twin runs: draw an
initial condition, flip one uniformly chosen cell, evolve both copies to the
horizon, and compare final rows. \emph{Damage survival} is the probability that
any difference remains; conditional on survival, \emph{damage fraction} is the
fraction of the ring that differs, \emph{spreading rate} is the damage-cone
extent normalised by $2r$ times the number of steps (so $1$ is light speed at
any radius), and \emph{cone fill} is the density of differing cells within the
cone (exact formulas in Appendix~\ref{app:methods}). Each target vector is a
Monte-Carlo average over $n_{\mathrm{pairs}}=256$ initial conditions from a
random stream keyed on the rule's identity, so a rule's target is a
well-defined function of (rule, protocol), reproducible under a fixed seed but
Monte-Carlo limited (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}).

\subsection{Three reliability benchmarks, distinguished}
\label{sec:reliability}
Because each cached target is a single Monte-Carlo estimate, no predictor can
agree with it perfectly. But ``the ceiling'' depends on \emph{what kind of
predictor} is being scored, and conflating the cases overstates or understates
a method by several times the margins at stake. From $K=20$ independent target
replicates per rule we report, per target, with rule-bootstrap confidence
intervals [registered, rev.~8]:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Latent-target reliability} $\mathrm{ICC}(1)=\sigma_a^2 /
(\sigma_a^2+\sigma_e^2)$ from a one-way random-effects decomposition
($\sigma_a^2$ between-rule signal, $\sigma_e^2$ Monte-Carlo noise): the
attenuation ceiling for a predictor of the \emph{noise-free} target mean.
\item \emph{Independent-replicate agreement}: the empirical \rtwo{} of one
replicate predicting another. For $Y_1=\theta+\varepsilon_1$,
$Y_2=\theta+\varepsilon_2$ with equal independent noise this is
$1-2\sigma_e^2/(\sigma_a^2+\sigma_e^2)=2\,\mathrm{ICC}-1$, \emph{not} ICC. This
is the correct benchmark for the mechanistic estimator below, which returns a
fresh independent simulation at the same $n_{\mathrm{pairs}}$---not the latent
mean.
\item \emph{Large-simulation reference}: the same estimator scored against
near-noise-free targets recomputed at $n_{\mathrm{pairs}}=4096$; as the
comparison target's noise vanishes, an optimal fresh-simulation estimator
should rise from benchmark (2) to benchmark (1).
\end{enumerate}

On ECA the four targets give ICC $0.994/0.999/0.9995/0.999$ (survival is the
noisiest, Monte-Carlo std $0.022$) and replicate agreement
$0.987/0.998/0.999/0.998$; on radius two, ICC $0.987/0.998/0.998/0.980$ and
agreement $0.974/0.997/0.997/0.959$. Survival and cone fill are markedly
heteroscedastic, so all intervals are bootstrap-based rather than Gaussian.
Every \rtwo{} in this paper is read against the benchmark appropriate to the
estimator's type.

\section{Estimators}
\label{sec:estimators}

\emph{(i) Matched-model system identification (``mechanistic'').} From one
diagram we tabulate every observed neighbourhood$\to$next-cell transition and
rebuild the $2^{2r+1}$-bit rule table, then re-run the twin-run protocol on the
reconstructed rule with an independent random stream. The estimator assumes
exactly the observation model above (deterministic synchronous binary CA, full
noiseless observation, known radius, spatial uniformity); it is \emph{not}
assumption-free, and it carries one explicit prior: table entries never
exercised by the diagram must be completed. The registered default completes
them with $0$; we report the observed \emph{coverage}, compare default-$0$,
default-$1$ and empirical-prior completions, and provide a posterior-averaged
variant that enumerates or samples the $2^k$ completions of $k$ missing entries
and propagates the spread into a predictive interval [registered, rev.~8].
Coverage is a non-issue in practice under this protocol---every ECA held-out
table and $156/160$ radius-two tables are fully covered; the four exceptions
miss one entry each, and the completion choice moves the radius-two median
\rtwo{} by ${<}\,0.001$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:results-mech}).

\emph{(ii) Five cheap statistics.} A regressor on five label-free
single-diagram features (polarity-folded density, temporal activity, zlib
compression ratio, $2\times2$ block entropy, and a radius-aware input-entropy
variance~\cite{wuensche1999}), all exactly or approximately invariant under the
orbit symmetries. Both a ridge and a gradient-boosted regressor are fit; the
stronger (the boosted one) is the baseline of record
(Appendix~\ref{app:methods}).

\emph{(iii) Direct convolutional amortiser.} A small CNN ($31{,}524$
parameters; full layer table in Appendix~\ref{app:methods}) whose $2\times2$
first-layer kernels cannot span a neighbourhood together with its output cell,
feeding a $64$-dimensional bottleneck and a linear head; BatchNorm keeps the
per-patch \texttt{predict\_map} variant local. Trained $60$ epochs at
$127\,$px; five seeds vary initialization and minibatch order. \emph{All neural
claims in this paper are about this architecture under this training budget},
not about deep networks as a class: we did not run an architecture search, and
Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion} names the learned system-identification pipelines
that remain untested. The ``anti-shortcut'' first layer is an architectural
intent only---Sec.~\ref{sec:probes} shows the rule remains decodable from the
trained bottleneck.

\emph{Statistical protocol.} Results are reported per target (never only as a
four-target median), with the held-out \emph{rule} as the unit of resampling.
Paired differences carry rule-level cluster-bootstrap intervals ($10^4$
resamples); each method pair is classified per target as \emph{superiority}
(median $\Delta R^2\ge0.10$, $95\%$ CI excluding $0$), \emph{practical
equivalence} (TOST: $90\%$ CI within $(-\delta,+\delta)$, $\delta=0.05$,
reported also at $\delta=0.02$ and $0.10$), or \emph{inconclusive}---the three
labels are not interchangeable and we use only the one earned. Split
uncertainty beyond the registered split is quantified for the deterministic
methods by leave-one-orbit-out over all $88$ ECA orbits and $20$ repeated outer
splits on radius two; the CNN is reported at the registered split with its
five-seed spread, and we state explicitly that retraining across splits was not
performed. A cross-fitted stacking test measures incremental \rtwo{} (margin
$0.02$, CI excluding $0$) in both directions between the network and the five
statistics.

\subsection{Rule reconstruction is simulation-limited}
\label{sec:results-mech}

Table~\ref{tab:headline} gives the headline medians; Tables~\ref{tab:eca}
and~\ref{tab:r2} give the per-target values with intervals on which every
claim rests. The mechanistic estimator recovers the rule exactly for $100\%$ of
ECA and $97.5\%$ of radius-two held-out rules and predicts each damage-response
statistic \emph{at its independent-replicate agreement benchmark}: on ECA,
survival $0.985$ against a benchmark of $0.987$ (CI $[0.978,0.992]$); on radius

model, reading the rule and re-running the experiment is statistically
indistinguishable from having re-measured the ground truth itself. Scored

the same predictions rise to the latent ICC ceiling (survival: ECA $0.994$
against ICC $0.994$; radius two $0.988$ against $0.987$)---the residual was
the comparison target's noise, not estimator error [registered, rev.~8].

The completion prior is exposed rather than hidden: coverage is complete for
every ECA held-out rule and for $156/160$ radius-two rules; the four
under-covered tables miss one entry each, the default-$0$/default-$1$/empirical
completions change the radius-two median \rtwo{} only in the fourth decimal
($0.9905$--$0.9908$), and the posterior-averaged variant supplies predictive
intervals precisely for those rules. We therefore describe the estimator as
carrying a weak, testable prior---not as assumption-free.

\subsection{No stable ordering between the direct estimators}
\label{sec:paired}

The paired comparisons [registered, rev.~7--8] give the network and the
five-statistic baseline no global ranking. On radius two ($160$ held-out rules,
adequately powered) the CNN is ahead on damage survival ($\Delta R^2=+0.08$,
$95\%$ CI $[+0.00,+0.20]$ excluding $0$ but below the $0.10$ superiority
margin), the baseline is \emph{superior} on cone fill ($\Delta R^2=-0.21$, CI
$[-0.38,-0.07]$), and fraction and rate are \emph{inconclusive} at
$\delta=0.05$ (equivalent only at the loose $\delta=0.10$; fraction's CI
$[-0.09,-0.00]$ weakly favours the baseline). On ECA ($18$ held-out orbits)
every CNN--baseline contrast is inconclusive except spreading rate (equivalent
at $\delta=0.05$); that panel is underpowered for tight per-target intervals
and we say so rather than read a ranking into it. The mechanistic estimator,
by contrast, meets the registered superiority rule against the baseline on
survival, rate and cone fill ($\Delta R^2$ up to $+0.39$) and against the CNN
on fraction, rate and cone fill (up to $+0.60$); the two remaining contrasts
(fraction vs.\ the baseline, survival vs.\ the CNN) have intervals excluding
zero in its favour with point differences just below the margin. No comparison
on any target in either space favours a direct method over it.

\subsection{What the network adds: complementarity, not redundancy}
\label{sec:stacking}

The cross-fitted stacking test [registered, rev.~7] asks the sharper question:
does one method's prediction explain held-out residual variance beyond
another's? The answer is target-specific (Table~\ref{tab:stacking}). Adding the
CNN's predictions to the five statistics yields a large, significant increment
on damage survival ($+0.32$, CI $[+0.23,+0.48]$)---raising the stacked model
from $0.56$ to $0.88$---with small significant increments on fraction and rate
and none on cone fill; the reverse direction adds nothing. The
network therefore extracts real survival information that texture statistics
miss---exactly the target where both direct methods sit farthest below the
reliability benchmark, and the concrete reason a learned representation cannot
be dismissed for this task family. It remains the case that the mechanistic
estimator's increment over the five statistics is far larger on every target
($+0.14$ to $+0.84$), so the practical order of preference under the matched
observation model is unambiguous.

\subsection{Why: the rule is readable, including from the network}
\label{sec:probes}

The mechanism behind these results is that the local rule pervades every
representation of the diagram. The deterministic inverter recovers it exactly
(above). Linear probes quantify the same fact for the learned and handcrafted
representations; we specify the two probe tasks precisely since they answer
different questions. \emph{Identity probes} (stratified split over diagrams;
train and test diagrams disjoint, rule classes shared---within-rule decoding
across new diagrams; $88$ and $800$ classes, majority baseline $\approx0.011$
and $0.001$) recover the rule from the CNN's own $64$-d bottleneck at balanced
accuracy $0.88$ (ECA) and $0.95$ (radius two), versus $0.65$/$0.30$ from the
five statistics. \emph{Truth-table-bit probes} (leave-rules-out GroupKFold;
test rules never seen by the probe---out-of-rule transfer of the reading
mechanism; chance $0.5$) reach $0.78$/$0.61$ from the bottleneck versus
$0.69$/$0.55$ from the statistics. The $2\times2$ first layer therefore does
\emph{not} prevent rule recovery; we retired that claim. What the probes
establish is accessibility, not use: they show the information survives into
the bottleneck, and the mechanistic estimator shows a rule-first route attains
the reliability benchmark. Whether the network's regression head \emph{causally
relies} on rule information is not established here; rule-scrubbing and
rule-provision interventions that would test it are future work
(Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\subsection{When reading fails: the identifiability phase diagram}
\label{sec:identifiability}

The mechanistic route is only as good as its observation model, so we mapped
its failure surface [exploratory, rev.~8]. Figure~\ref{fig:ident} sweeps, on
the radius-two held-out rules, four observation axes; per setting it reports
table coverage, exact-reconstruction rate, and the median held-out \rtwo{} of
the mechanistic estimator, the five statistics, and the \emph{frozen} CNN (no
retraining; axes that change the input geometry omit the CNN, as noted).

\label{fig:ident}

Three regimes emerge. Where the observation model is matched (clean, full,
mid-density), reconstruction is exact after a handful of rows and the
mechanistic estimator holds its benchmark---a regime in which single-diagram
amortization of this task family has no room to add value. Under degradation
the picture inverts: at $5\%$ bit-flip noise the mechanistic median \rtwo{}
falls below zero while the frozen CNN retains $0.09$--$0.43$; under masking the
crossover sits near $40\%$; at density $0.1$ all methods fail but none
catastrophically like the inverter under noise. The boundary is sharp because a
deterministic table has no noise model---one corrupted transition flips an
entry. A rule-\emph{posterior} simulator (our posterior-averaged variant with a
transition-error likelihood) is the natural extension in the intermediate
regime, and a learned rule-reader may be the natural one beyond it; neither is
benchmarked here. These curves are the paper's scope statement in graphical
form: the recommendation to read the rule holds \emph{inside} panel (a)'s
regime and is explicitly not asserted outside it.

\subsection{Cost: accuracy versus compute}
\label{sec:compute}

Accuracy is not the only utility criterion. The mechanistic estimator pays per
query (rule inference plus a fresh Monte-Carlo simulation), the network pays
once (training) and then almost nothing per query. Measured on radius two
[exploratory, rev.~8]: at the production budget ($256$ pairs) the mechanistic
estimator costs $283$\,ms per diagram (single CPU core; embarrassingly
parallel over diagrams), against $2.2$\,ms for a CNN forward pass (Metal GPU)
and $1.4$\,ms for the five statistics. One-time costs are $\approx22$\,min of
training per CNN seed versus $15$\,s to fit the boosted baseline, giving a
break-even of $\approx4700$ queries before the network's training amortises
against the mechanistic estimator. The accuracy-versus-budget curve, however,
is one-sided: even at a $16$-pair budget ($17$\,ms per diagram) the mechanistic
median \rtwo{} is $0.924$---above both direct estimators at any cost---and
$32$ pairs ($32$\,ms) already give $0.966$. The choice is therefore
workload-dependent in the ordinary way; what the Pareto view adds is that
under the matched observation model the mechanistic estimator is
simultaneously the most accurate at every simulation budget we measured, so
the network's case must rest on degraded-observation regimes
(Fig.~\ref{fig:ident}) or on extreme-throughput screening, not on accuracy.

\subsection{Descriptive by-products, independently validated}
\label{sec:constructive}

\emph{Phenotype maps for non-uniform CA.} Because the statistics are locally
predictable, sliding the pretrained five-statistic regressor over a composed
(two-rule) diagram yields an interpretable phenotype map with no additional
training at deployment (the regressor itself is trained once; we do not call
this training-free). Scored against an independent location-resolved ground
truth---the damage response measured by local perturbation \emph{in} the
composed system---with the window width chosen on a validation split of rule
pairs and the \emph{composed system} as the unit of resampling, the learned
map's advantage is \emph{inconclusive} under the registered framework: mean
Spearman $0.67$ vs $0.61$, $\Delta=+0.047$, $95\%$ CI $[-0.002,+0.102]$, $90\%$
CI $[+0.005,+0.092]$---not superiority (point below the $0.10$ margin), not
equivalence (the $90\%$ CI is not contained in $\pm0.05$). The honest summary
is a small, statistically unresolved edge for the learned map at $15$-column
resolution.

\emph{A damage-signature landscape of the radius-two space.} The same
statistics over $3000$ uniformly sampled canonical rules give
Fig.~\ref{fig:landscape}: $7.8\%$ (Wilson $95\%$ CI $[6.9,8.8]\%$) of rules
satisfy the registered damage-signature criterion (persistent, sub-ballistic,
sparse-cone damage), versus $2/88$ elementary orbits. We emphasize what this
is: the prevalence of a \emph{finite-horizon damage-signature criterion} under
the stated sampling distribution---not a validated glider census. On ECA,
where literature ground truth exists, the signature earns considerable trust:
it selects $\{54,106,110\}$, i.e.\ both canonical class-IV rules (sensitivity
$1.0$) with one false positive, the documented borderline rule $106$ (precision
$2/3$, specificity $0.99$, balanced accuracy $0.99$). An \emph{independent}
localized-seed glider detector is far weaker as an instrument (vs.\ literature:
sensitivity $0.5$---it misses rule $54$, whose ether front advances at light
speed---precision $0.17$); on radius two it agrees with the signature at
barely better than chance (balanced accuracy $0.39$, precision $0.05$, $231$
applicable rules). Single-observable glider detection is unreliable in the
large space, which is precisely why we do not promote the $7.8\%$ region to
``complex'' or ``glider-supporting'': that would require long-horizon curated
validation that we have not done.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Under a fully observed, noiseless, deterministic, synchronous, binary
observation model with known radius, a single space--time diagram identifies
the local rule almost surely, and reconstructing the generator then re-running
the stipulated experiment predicts finite-horizon damage response at the
accuracy of re-measuring it. That is the central, positive, and--- we
believe---generalizable finding: \emph{a nominally single-observation
amortization benchmark can be solved by system identification whenever the
observation encodes the generator}, and benchmark designers who intend to
test representation learning must break that encoding (noise, partial
observation, unknown model class) or accept that they are benchmarking
identification.

The neural results are deliberately scoped. For \emph{this} constrained
architecture at \emph{this} training budget, direct regression neither reaches
the reliability benchmark nor dominates five cheap statistics, and the two
trade places by target; the network's one demonstrated edge---substantial
incremental survival information ($+0.32$)---is real, is exactly where texture
is least informative, and survives cross-fitting. We do not conclude that deep
networks cannot close the gap. The probes show rule information is accessible
to the network; whether exploiting it is an architecture, objective, or
optimization question is open, and the natural next comparators are learned
system identifiers---a rule-table prediction head feeding a differentiable or
exact simulator---plus rule-scrubbing and rule-provision interventions to test
causality, and evaluation in the degraded-observation regimes of
Fig.~\ref{fig:ident} where exact inversion is unavailable and a learned reader
would compete against a rule-posterior simulator rather than against a solved
problem.

Every claim is bounded by the protocol tuple (Bernoulli$(1/2)$ ICs, ring
$127$, radius-aware horizon): the statistics are finite-horizon and
measure-dependent by construction~\cite{gilman1987,culikyu1988}, and
Fig.~\ref{fig:ident}(d) exhibits that dependence directly. Within that scope
the practical guidance is plain and now carries its price tag
(Sec.~\ref{sec:compute}): if the observation model is matched, read the rule
and simulate; if observations are noisy, partial, or off-distribution, the
identification shortcut degrades sharply and learned estimators---including
hybrid statistics-plus-network stacks for survival-like targets---are the live
option. For the broader program of behaviour-first (phenotype) analysis of
CA~\cite{wolfram2002,lipackard1990,israeli2006}, the damage-signature
landscape and the composed-system maps stand as usable, independently
validated artefacts, described at the strength the validation supports and no
stronger~\cite{kleinberg2002,mitchell1993}.

\section{Reproducibility and registration status}
\label{app:repro}

All experiments run on one Apple-silicon machine (Metal/CPU, fp32); the
pipeline is first-party except PyTorch and scikit-learn. Targets, splits and
panels are keyed on fixed seeds; the mechanistic estimator and reliability
replicates use random streams provably independent of the target cache (prime
seed offsets on per-rule \texttt{SeedSequence}s), so agreement between them is
never contamination. All decision rules, margins and panels were committed to
a timestamped private repository before the corresponding measurements; the
full history is released with the article. Because an in-review repository is
not independently auditable, we label every analysis here rather than lean on
the word ``pre-registered'': \emph{registered} (rule committed numbers-free
before measurement): the damage-signature criterion, holdout split, paired
inference and TOST margins, stacking margin, reliability design, mechanistic
estimator and completion policy, probe design, detector-validation design,
map ground-truth design; \emph{post-hoc confirmatory} (analysis added at
revision, on data or rules not used to tune it): the replicate-agreement
correction, the large-simulation reference, confusion-matrix reporting, TOST
margin sensitivity; \emph{exploratory}: the identifiability phase diagram and
the accuracy--compute curve. One deviation from the original registration is
disclosed: an early implementation seeded target streams by panel position
rather than rule identity (cross-panel drift up to $0.05$--$0.08$ on
borderline-survival rules); it was replaced by identity keying and the drift
verified to vanish.

\section{Methods supplement}
\label{app:methods}

\emph{Data.} ECA: all $88$ orbit representatives, $256$ diagrams per rule;
radius two: $800$ canonical rules ($857$ drawn, deduplicated by the order-four
reflect/complement orbit), $64$ diagrams per rule; all diagrams
$127\times127$, Bernoulli$(1/2)$ ICs, no transient discarded. Observed
diagrams and target-estimation streams are disjoint by construction (dataset
seed vs.\ target seed), and no random numbers are shared across methods.
Splits: leave-rules-out, holdout fraction $0.2$, stratified by Wolfram label
where available, seed $0$; radius two force-holds the $57$ signature-complex
panel rules (so the complex subset is never trained on); ECA force-holds rule
$110$ and force-trains rule $54$. Hyperparameters were tuned only on training
rules.

\emph{Targets.} With flip position $x_0$ uniform on the ring, horizon
$T=\lfloor w/(2r)\rfloor-1$, damage indicator $d=\mathbb{1}[a_T\ne b_T]$:
survival $=\Pr[\exists i: d_i=1]$; fraction $=\mathbb{E}[\bar d\mid\text{surv}]$;
rate $=\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{ext}(d)/(2rT)\mid\text{surv}]$ with $\mathrm{ext}$
the re-centred cone extent (no wrap by construction); fill
$=\mathbb{E}[\sum_i d_i/\mathrm{ext}(d)\mid\text{surv}]$. Conditional targets
are $0$ when no pair survives (itself the ordered-rule signature); surviving
trial counts therefore vary by rule, one reason survival-adjacent targets are
heteroscedastic and all reliability intervals are bootstrapped.

\emph{Network.} Input $1\times127\times127$; four stages of [Conv $2\times2$,
stride 1, no padding $\to$ BatchNorm $\to$ ReLU $\to$ MaxPool $2\times2$] with
channels $16/32/64/64$; $1\times1$ convolution to a $64$-d bottleneck with
BatchNorm and no final nonlinearity; global average pool; linear head to $4$
targets ($31{,}524$ parameters; receptive field $31\times31$ per bottleneck
position before pooling). AdamW, lr $10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-6}$, batch
$128$, $60$ epochs, no early stopping; targets standardized on training rules.
Augmentations: cyclic shift, horizontal flip, polarity inversion (the exact
orbit symmetries). Training cost $\approx22$\,min (radius two) and
$\approx10$\,min (ECA) per seed on the reference machine.

\emph{Baselines.} Ridge ($\alpha=1$) and scikit-learn
\texttt{GradientBoostingRegressor} (defaults, one per target) on the five
features standardized on training rules; the boosted model is stronger on both
spaces and is the baseline of record. Split sensitivity of the baseline:
leave-one-orbit-out over the $88$ ECA orbits gives per-target \rtwo{} $0.73$
(survival), $0.85$ (fraction), $0.95$ (rate), $0.93$ (fill), consistent with
the registered-split values; over $20$ repeated radius-two outer splits the
per-target median (5th--95th percentile) is $0.76$ $(0.56$--$0.84)$ for
survival, $0.92$ $(0.90$--$0.93)$ for fraction, $0.90$ $(0.87$--$0.91)$ for
rate and $0.68$ $(0.55$--$0.75)$ for cone fill---the noisy targets carry
substantial split variance, one more reason per-target intervals accompany
every claim.

\emph{Verdicts at other margins.} At $\delta=0.02$ the single equivalence
verdict (ECA spreading rate, CNN vs.\ baseline) reverts to inconclusive; at
$\delta=0.10$ radius-two fraction and rate additionally become equivalent; no
superiority verdict changes at any margin, so the ``no stable ordering''
conclusion is margin-insensitive.

\emph{Panels.} The $800$-rule estimator panel is a subset of the $3000$-rule
landscape sample (same sampling stream); landscape prevalence is therefore
computed on the superset and estimator results never touch the extra $2200$
rules. The reliability panel subsamples $250$ of the $800$ (fixed seed).

\emph{Detector.} The localized-seed detector evolves a single seeded cell on a
quiescent background (ring $255$ confirmation) and flags persistent, localized,
sub-ballistic, non-space-filling structures (thresholds fixed in code before
radius-two application: alive fraction $\ge0.5$, growth rate $0.05$--$0.9$,
fill $\le0.75$). Its failure modes on ECA are documented in
Sec.~\ref{sec:constructive}; a multi-observable detector (periodicity,
collisions, comoving localization) is the obvious upgrade and was not built
here.

\emph{Composed-system maps.} Half/half mosaics over the $16$-rule panel listed
in the released configuration; pairs with spreading-rate gap $\ge0.25$; $8$
ICs per pair; local ground truth by single-cell perturbation at the map's $15$
column centres with a $24$-step local horizon and $64$ pairs per location,
independent streams. Window width selected once on the even-indexed pairs;
odd-indexed pairs are the test set; the per-pair best window appears only as a
labelled oracle sensitivity ($0.638$, between the validation-selected $0.605$
and the CNN's $0.673$).
