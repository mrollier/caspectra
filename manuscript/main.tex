\begin{abstract}
The behaviour of a cellular automaton (CA) is most defensibly characterised not
by its update rule but by \emph{dynamical invariants}---quantities such as
damage spreading that are defined through perturbation experiments (evolve two
copies of a configuration that differ in one cell and watch the difference
grow). Such invariants require running the simulator twice, so estimating them
from a single observed space--time diagram is a natural task for a learned
amortiser, and a natural place to ask whether deep representation learning adds
value over hand-crafted features. We answer this with a pre-registered
benchmark. Across the elementary rule space and an enlarged two-state,
radius-two space of $2^{32}$ rules, we compare an anti-shortcut convolutional
amortiser against a regressor on five cheap, label-free single-diagram
statistics, under an explicit observation protocol and leave-rules-out
evaluation. The deep network provides \emph{no advantage}: the simple baseline
matches or exceeds it at predicting the invariants globally, at placing held-out
\emph{complex} (glider-supporting) rules once such rules are made plentiful, and
at resolving behaviour \emph{spatially} in non-uniform CA. We explain why---the
local rule is readable from the diagram and the invariants are strongly
texture-correlated---and turn the negative into two constructive results: a
training-free, interpretable method for spatially-resolved phenotype maps of
non-uniform CA, and a data-driven map of where complex behaviour lives in rule
space (${\approx}\,7.8\%$ of radius-two rules, versus ${\approx}\,2\%$ of
elementary rules). Pre-registration, code, and data are released.
\end{abstract}

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Rule spaces and the observation protocol}
Elementary CA (ECA; two states, radius one, 256 rules, 88 reflection/complement
orbit representatives) and an enlarged \emph{radius-two} space (two states,
five-cell neighbourhood, $2^{32}$ rules), sampled and orbit-deduplicated.
Protocol tuple: Bernoulli$(1/2)$ initial conditions, ring of $127$ cells,
horizon $127$ rows (radius-aware for range two). TODO: expand.

\subsection{Damage-spreading invariants}
Four label-free scalars per rule from twin runs (flip one IC cell, evolve both):
\emph{damage survival}, \emph{damage fraction}, \emph{spreading rate}
(normalised so $1=$ light speed), \emph{cone fill}. The horizon caps the light
cone so it cannot wrap. Targets are cached per rule under a fixed protocol with
$n_{\mathrm{pairs}}=256$; each rule's random stream is keyed on its identity so
a rule's target is a well-defined function of $(\text{rule},\text{protocol})$
independent of the panel it is computed in (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}). TODO.

\subsection{The deep amortiser and the simple baseline}
\emph{Amortiser}: an anti-shortcut CNN (2$\times$2 first-layer kernels, a signed
64-d bottleneck) with a linear regression head, and a \texttt{predict\_map}
variant that emits per-patch predictions; BatchNorm (inference-time running
statistics) makes the map genuinely local. \emph{Baseline}: ridge and
gradient-boosted-tree regressors on five single-diagram statistics---folded
density, temporal activity, compression ratio, $2\times2$ block entropy, and a
radius-aware input-entropy variance (Wuensche's glider
screen~\cite{wuensche1999})---all invariant under the orbit symmetries. Both are
trained on the same rules and evaluated leave-rules-out. TODO.

\subsection{The complexity landscape and complex-rule placement}
\label{sec:complex}
Under uniform sampling, complex (glider-supporting) rules are
${\approx}\,7.8\%$ of the radius-two space (Wilson 95\% CI $[6.9,8.8]\%$;
$n=3000$), versus ${\approx}\,2\%$ of the 88 ECA orbits---so the intermediate
regime, a two-member club among ECAs, is genuinely populated. Held out entirely,
these complex rules are placed \emph{without systematic bias}---but by the
\emph{baseline} as well as the amortiser (Table~\ref{tab:global}, last row):
populating the regime, not the network, is what removes the bias
(Fig.~\ref{fig:landscape}).

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
Why the negative is structural, not a tuning artefact: (i) the local rule is
recoverable from the diagram~\cite{rollier2024cnn}, and (ii) the damage
invariants are strongly correlated with visible texture, so a handful of cheap
statistics capture what the CNN captures. A forecasting objective would inherit
the same shortcut (a recovered rule forecasts perfectly). The constructive
upshot: cheap, interpretable, training-free phenotype maps for non-uniform CA,
and a data-driven complexity landscape. Relation to shortcut
learning~\cite{geirhos2020shortcut} and to the impossibility of a bias-free
taxonomy~\cite{kleinberg2002}. Limits: this invariant family and protocol; other
invariants (e.g.\ genuinely non-local ones) may yet reward learning. TODO.
