\begin{abstract}
Damage-response statistics of a cellular automaton (CA), whether and how far
the difference between two runs differing in one cell spreads, are defined
through \emph{twin} simulations, so estimating them from one observed diagram
is a natural task for learned amortized models. We benchmark that task under
pre-committed decision rules, over the $88$ elementary-CA orbits and $800$
sampled radius-two rules, for complete, noiseless, binary diagrams of known
radius. Under these assumptions a single $127$-cell diagram identifies the
rule table for $100\%$ (elementary) and $97.5\%$ (radius-two) of held-out
rules, after which re-running the perturbation experiment is
\emph{exchangeable} with an independent Monte-Carlo replicate of the target:
agreement at the replicate benchmark follows by construction, so the matched
regime measures the reconstruction rate, whose residual failures prove
dynamically benign. Separating this simulation-limited benchmark
($2\,\mathrm{ICC}-1$) from the latent-reliability ceiling (ICC), we report
errors in units of the target's own Monte-Carlo error $\rho$, whose two
ceilings are exactly $\sqrt2$ and $1$: reconstruct-and-simulate holds
$\rho\le1.8$ where two direct learned estimators reach $\rho=3$--$33$, and
their apparent skill on damage survival is largely mode assignment---within
the intermediate survival band both fall below zero \rtwo{} on the elementary
panel. An identifiability frontier maps where rule reading fails: a
pseudo-posterior decoder extends it to $50\%$ masking, extreme initial
densities and ${\sim}3\%$ noise, and the noise band beyond belongs to
whichever estimator was trained for the corruption. Because that decoder
models output-side flips only, those noise-band verdicts bound the estimators
evaluated, not the read-then-simulate family. Wherever the rule is
recoverable, no tested direct estimator matches reading it, not even an
unconstrained network $354\times$ larger. When the observation encodes the
generator, system identification, whose accuracy is set by simulation budget
rather than training, is the baseline to beat.
\end{abstract}

\maketitle

\begin{quotation}
Machine learning is increasingly used to read dynamical properties---chaos,
stability, sensitivity to perturbation---directly off a single picture of a
system's evolution, skipping the perturbation experiments that define those
properties. Cellular automata are the cleanest laboratory for asking what
such shortcuts really measure, because the picture often contains enough
information to rebuild the generating rule itself. We make that alternative
explicit: rebuild the rule from one diagram, re-run the perturbation
experiment, and compare. Under a matched observation model this transparent
route is as accurate as re-measuring the ground truth, while two learned
models fall well short of it. Degrading the observation reveals an orderly
frontier where exact reconstruction fails, probabilistic variants extend it,
and beyond them the race is won by whichever estimator was trained for the
corruption at hand. The result is a calibrated, pre-registered template for
fair baselines in data-driven analysis of dynamical systems, and a map of
when each strategy is the right one.
\end{quotation}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Cellular automata (CA) are the canonical minimal model of emergent computation:
a single local update rule, iterated in parallel, produces the full zoo of
behaviours from frozen order to chaos to the localized interacting ``particles''
of complex rules~\cite{wolfram1984,wolfram2002,langton1990,boccara1991,rollier2025taxonomy}. Classifying that behaviour is a
long-standing and subtle problem, because the field distinguishes the
\emph{genotype}---the local update rule---from the \emph{phenotype}---the
mesoscopic structure of the spacetime diagram it
generates~\cite{vispoel2022progress,rollier2025taxonomy}. A method intended
to read behaviour off the diagram must be prevented from instead reading the
\emph{rule} off it: whenever the diagram exercises the table under the
assumed deterministic, synchronous update it contains, in its local
neighbourhoods, the entire rule table---CA and convolutional networks are
formally close~\cite{gilpin2019}---and a convolutional classifier recovers
the elementary-rule label from a single clean diagram with $99.9\%$ test
accuracy unless architecturally impeded~\cite{rollier2024cnn}. This is an instance of
\emph{shortcut learning}~\cite{geirhos2020shortcut} in its cleanest form.

One natural formalization of Wolfram-style class membership is
undecidable~\cite{culikyu1988}---the obstruction applies to that asymptotic
formalization, not to every finite protocol---and the class observed in
practice depends on the initial-condition measure~\cite{gilman1987}, the
lattice, and the horizon. The defensible object
of study is therefore not ``the class of a rule'' but a \emph{protocol
tuple}---(rule orbit, IC measure, lattice, horizon)---characterized by
finite-horizon, perturbation-defined statistics. The best-posed of these is
\emph{damage spreading}: evolve two copies of a configuration that differ in a
single cell and measure how the difference grows. The construction has a long
lineage---Kauffman's damage experiments on Boolean
networks~\cite{kauffman1969}, the annealed treatment of Derrida and
Pomeau~\cite{derridapomeau1986}, and its formalization for CA, where the
multiplication rate of defects, counted via Boolean derivatives, defines a
maximal Lyapunov exponent~\cite{bagnoli1992,baetens2010}. Our targets are the finite-horizon,
protocol-indexed versions of exactly these
quantities~\cite{vispoel2024damage,vispoel2022progress}: we call them
\emph{damage-response statistics}, not invariants, because they are
finite-size, finite-horizon and measure-dependent by construction.

Because damage-response statistics require \emph{two} runs of the simulator,
they are not a function of any single observed diagram, which makes them a
natural target for \emph{amortization} in the sense of simulation-based
inference---pay for training once, then answer per observation at almost no
cost~\cite{cranmer2020}. Benchmarks of this shape, in which a learned model
predicts a dynamical property directly from an observation of a
deterministic system, are increasingly common, and comparisons against
strong classical baselines are documented to be frequently absent or weak in
that literature~\cite{mcgreivy2024}. Under the
observation model in which such benchmarks are usually posed---the diagram is
complete, noiseless, binary, generated by a deterministic synchronous CA of
known radius---the strong baseline is a transparent alternative with
thirty-five years of prior art: \emph{infer the rule from the diagram and
simulate the statistic}. Identifying a binary CA from spacetime data by
tabulating observed transitions is an established inverse problem, essentially
solved under matched assumptions---posed for experimental data by Richards,
Meyer and Packard~\cite{richards1990}, treated at monograph length by
Adamatzky~\cite{adamatzky1994}, and still active in modern
variants~\cite{yangbillings2000,sunrosinmartin2011,elser2021,mordvintsev2020};
Table~\ref{tab:priorwork} tabulates each line's assumptions and is where our
novelty boundary is auditable. We claim no
novelty for the inverter; what has not been measured is what this
thirty-five-year-old fact does to the amortization benchmark. The premise is
in part our own prior result, and we state the delta plainly rather than leave
it to be inferred: Ref.~\onlinecite{rollier2024cnn} showed that a network
\emph{reads the rule} off a clean elementary diagram; this paper measures the
\emph{consequence} of that readability for a perturbation-defined benchmark,
calibrates the resulting predictions against replicate reliability, and maps
where the readability itself ends. One structural
consequence deserves stating up front. Because the targets are
simulation-defined, identification reproduces the target's own measurement
procedure: at the target's Monte-Carlo budget it attains the
independent-replicate benchmark \emph{by construction}, at a larger budget it
climbs to the latent ceiling. Identification therefore yields a
\emph{budget-indexed family} of estimators, not a point, and the
$1-\mathrm{ICC}$ gap between those benchmarks (Sec.~\ref{sec:reliability}) is
the entire noisy-score headroom any direct estimator holds over the family's
matched-budget member; a parallel cost hierarchy is known on the rule-table
side~\cite{vispoel2026structure}. Wherever the rule is exactly
recoverable, direct amortization can therefore win only on cost, with
simulation budget now part of the cost, or on robustness to observation
degradation. What remains empirical is how close identification comes to its
benchmark at matched budget, whether the learned estimators recover the
latent mean or merely fit the noisy cache, and where the matched regime
ends. Three results organize everything that follows.

\emph{First}, single-diagram identification, stipulated as prior art, is
confirmed at scale ($100\%$ of elementary and $97.5\%$ of sampled radius-two
held-out rules recovered exactly). The contribution is not the inverter but
the calibration: conditional on exact recovery the agreement with the target
is an \emph{identity} (Sec.~\ref{sec:results-mech}), so the matched regime's
empirical content is the reconstruction rate, and what needs stating is a
benchmark that knows the difference between ``as good as re-measuring the
target'' and ``as good as the truth'' (Sec.~\ref{sec:reliability}).
\emph{Second}, the two direct estimators we trained fall well short of that
benchmark, by $3$ to $33$ replicate-noise units, and where they appear closest
---damage survival---the skill is largely mode assignment rather than
resolution within the intermediate band. \emph{Third}, the mechanistic route
is brittle outside its assumptions: exact reconstruction collapses under
modest noise, heavy masking, or extreme initial-condition density
(Sec.~\ref{sec:identifiability}), and the estimator race across that frontier
(Sec.~\ref{sec:frontier}) shows what replaces it---a pseudo-posterior decoder
in the intermediate masking and density band, and beyond ${\sim}3\%$ noise
whichever estimator is trained for the corruption. Wherever the rule is
recoverable, no tested direct estimator beats reading it, not even an
unconstrained network $354\times$ larger. The frontier, not the
matched-regime verdict, is what transfers.

Decision rules, margins and rule panels were committed to a timestamped
repository before the corresponding measurements; Appendix~\ref{app:repro} is
the per-analysis record, and labels are not repeated inline.

\section{Benchmark}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Rule spaces and the observation model}
We use two rule spaces. \emph{Elementary CA} (ECA): two states, radius one,
$2^{2^3}=256$ rules, of which $88$ are distinct representatives under the
reflection and complementation symmetries a behavioural method should
respect~\cite{lipackard1990}---the \emph{independent rules} of the CA
taxonomy~\cite{rollier2025taxonomy}; we call each such equivalence class an
\emph{orbit}.
\emph{Radius-two CA}, an extended-neighbourhood
CA~\cite{rollier2025taxonomy}: two states, a five-cell neighbourhood, a $32$-entry table
and $2^{32}\approx4\times10^{9}$ rules; ECA embeds exactly (a continuity
control). The radius-two space is sampled uniformly ($800$ canonical rules after
orbit deduplication). Uniform sampling over encodings is a defensible
\emph{statistical} measure and the one under which every radius-two prevalence
here is stated, but it is a poor \emph{dynamical} one: a uniformly drawn
Boolean function of five inputs is overwhelmingly chaotic-looking, so
structured rules are rare by construction. This is why the estimator panel
force-holds the signature-complex rules (Sec.~\ref{sec:results-mech}); a
$\lambda$-stratified draw over table densities would sample the interesting
region directly and is the better design for a successor study.

Every quantity is indexed by a fixed \emph{observation protocol}: a
Bernoulli$(1/2)$ initial condition on a ring of $127$ cells (periodic
boundary conditions; non-power-of-two, so additive rules do not collapse to
homogeneity), evolved from $t=0$; the
damage horizon is $\lfloor w/(2r)\rfloor-1$ steps so a perturbation cone cannot
wrap. The observation model deliberately matches the generator: diagrams are
\emph{complete, noiseless, binary, synchronous in the observed time direction,
spatially uniform in the rule, and of known radius}. These assumptions are load
bearing---they are what make the rule readable---and
Sec.~\ref{sec:identifiability} measures what happens when each is relaxed.

\subsection{Damage-response statistics}
The targets are four scalars per rule, estimated from twin runs: draw an
initial condition, flip one uniformly chosen cell, evolve both copies to the
horizon, and compare final rows. \emph{Damage survival} is the probability that
any difference remains; conditional on survival, \emph{damage fraction} is the
fraction of the ring that differs, \emph{spreading rate} is the damage-cone
extent normalized by $2r$ times the number of steps, and \emph{cone fill} is
the density of differing cells within the cone (exact formulas in
Appendix~\ref{app:methods}). Two conventions deserve stating. Because extent
counts endpoints, the light-cone maximum is $(2rT+1)/(2rT)=1.008$ rather than
$1$, so ``rate $1$'' is light speed up to one cell of extent; the largest rate
we observe anywhere is $0.816$, so nothing in this paper sits near that
boundary. And a single surviving damaged cell has extent $1$ and hence fill
$1$, placing maximally sparse damage at the \emph{top} of the fill axis
alongside a solid cone; this degeneracy affects $0.13\%$ of the sampled
radius-two rules (and the embedded ordered ECAs $204$ and $184$), which is why
we report fill beside the spreading rate rather than alone. In the
Bagnoli--Rechtman--Ruffo construction the maximal Lyapunov exponent is defined
by the \emph{multiplication rate} of defects~\cite{bagnoli1992,baetens2010},
which our fraction and rate track; survival is the coarser finite-horizon
\emph{proxy} for that exponent's sign, i.e.\ for positivity of the damage
measure---the asymptotic exponent itself is not defined by any finite
protocol. Each target vector is a
Monte-Carlo average over $n_{\mathrm{pairs}}=256$ initial conditions from a
random stream keyed on the rule's identity, so a rule's target is a
well-defined function of (rule, protocol), reproducible under a fixed seed but
Monte-Carlo limited (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}).

\subsection{Three reliability benchmarks, distinguished}
\label{sec:reliability}
Because each cached target is a single Monte-Carlo estimate, no predictor
can agree with it perfectly, and ``the ceiling'' depends on \emph{what kind
of predictor} is being scored: conflating the cases misstates a method by
several times the margins at stake. From $K=20$ independent target
replicates per rule we report, per target, with rule-bootstrap confidence
intervals:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Latent-target reliability} $\mathrm{ICC}(1)=\sigma_a^2 /
(\sigma_a^2+\sigma_e^2)$ from a one-way random-effects decomposition
($\sigma_a^2$ between-rule signal, $\sigma_e^2$ Monte-Carlo noise): the
attenuation ceiling for a predictor of the \emph{noise-free} target mean.
\item \emph{Independent-replicate agreement}: the empirical \rtwo{} of one
replicate predicting another. Under the idealization
$Y_1=\theta+\varepsilon_1$, $Y_2=\theta+\varepsilon_2$ with equal,
independent, additive noise this is
$1-2\sigma_e^2/(\sigma_a^2+\sigma_e^2)=2\,\mathrm{ICC}-1$, \emph{not} ICC.
This is the appropriate benchmark for a correctly identified rule followed by
an independent simulation at the same $n_{\mathrm{pairs}}$---an estimator
that returns a fresh Monte-Carlo draw, not the latent mean.
\item \emph{Large-simulation reference}: the same estimator scored against
near-noise-free targets recomputed at $n_{\mathrm{pairs}}=4096$; as the
comparison target's noise vanishes, an optimal fresh-simulation estimator
should rise from benchmark (2) to benchmark (1).
\end{enumerate}

The gap between the two ceilings, $\mathrm{ICC}-(2\,\mathrm{ICC}-1) =
1-\mathrm{ICC}$, is the entire noisy-score headroom available to \emph{any}
estimator over a matched-budget fresh simulation; identification claims it by
raising its own $n_{\mathrm{pairs}}$, a direct model only by actually
estimating $\theta$. Because survival and cone fill are markedly
heteroscedastic across rules, we do not lean on the Gaussian identity:
benchmark (2) is \emph{measured} as the mean pairwise \rtwo{} over the $K=20$
replicates (the identity is reported as a cross-check), ICC(1) comes from the
one-way variance decomposition used descriptively, and every interval is a
rule-level bootstrap rather than a Gaussian standard error. The measured
values are the rightmost columns of Tables~\ref{tab:eca}
and~\ref{tab:r2}; survival is the noisiest target (ECA Monte-Carlo std
$0.022$).

Reading \rtwo{} against a different ceiling per target and per panel is
error-prone, so we also report each estimator's error in units of the target's
own Monte-Carlo error,
\begin{equation}
\rho_t \;=\;
\sqrt{\frac{\sum_i\bigl(\hat Y_{ti}-Y_{1,ti}\bigr)^2}{\sum_i\hat\sigma_e^2(t,i)}},
\label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}
with $\hat\sigma_e(t,i)$ estimated \emph{per rule} from the $K=20$ replicates.
Pooling the two sums, rather than averaging per-rule ratios, weights each rule
by its own Monte-Carlo variance and is what makes the two reference values
exact rather than approximate; the two ceilings above become constants: an exactly identified rule resimulated at
matched budget gives $\rho=\sqrt2$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:exchangeable}, and a
perfect predictor of the latent mean gives $\rho=1$. Unlike \rtwo{}, $\rho$ is
invariant to panel composition, is bounded below by $0$ with no sign
pathology, and states directly how many replicate-noise units an estimator is
off. We keep \rtwo{} as the primary column for comparability with the
literature and report $\rho$ alongside it.

\section{Estimators}
\label{sec:estimators}

\emph{(i) Matched-model system identification (``mechanistic'').} From one
diagram we tabulate every observed neighbourhood$\to$next-cell transition
over all consecutive row pairs (the $t{=}0$ row included as an input row,
periodic in space) and rebuild the $2^{2r+1}$-bit rule table, then re-run the
twin-run protocol on the reconstructed rule with an independent random
stream. Conflicting observations of the same neighbourhood---impossible for
a clean deterministic CA, routine under the degradations of
Sec.~\ref{sec:identifiability}---are resolved by \emph{majority vote} (ties
to $0$), with an inconsistency flag; pseudocode for this and every degraded
decoder is in Appendix~\ref{app:algorithms}. The estimator assumes exactly the
observation model above; it is \emph{not} assumption-free, and it carries
one explicit prior: table entries never exercised by the diagram must be
completed. The registered default completes
them with $0$; we report the observed \emph{coverage}, compare default-$0$,
default-$1$ and empirical-prior completions, and provide a posterior-averaged
variant that enumerates or samples the $2^k$ completions of $k$ missing entries
and propagates the spread into a predictive interval.
Coverage proves a non-issue under this protocol at either evaluation unit
(Sec.~\ref{sec:results-mech}).

\emph{(ii) Five cheap statistics.} A regressor on five label-free
single-diagram features (polarity-folded density, temporal activity, zlib
compression ratio~\cite{zenil2010}, $2\times2$ block entropy, and a
radius-aware input-entropy
variance~\cite{wuensche1999}), all exactly or approximately invariant under the
orbit symmetries. Both a ridge and a gradient-boosted regressor are fit; the
stronger (the boosted one) is the baseline of record
(Appendix~\ref{app:methods}).

\emph{(iii) Direct convolutional amortizer.} A small CNN ($31{,}524$
parameters; full layer table in Appendix~\ref{app:methods}) whose $2\times2$
first-layer kernels cannot span a neighbourhood together with its output cell,
feeding a $64$-dimensional bottleneck and a linear head; BatchNorm keeps the
per-patch \texttt{predict\_map} variant local. Trained $60$ epochs at
$127\,$px; five seeds vary initialization and minibatch order. \emph{All neural
claims in this paper are about this architecture under this training budget},
not about deep networks as a class: we did not run an architecture search, and
Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion} names the learned system-identification pipelines
that remain untested. The ``anti-shortcut'' first layer is an architectural
intent only---Sec.~\ref{sec:probes} shows the rule remains decodable from the
trained bottleneck. Two registered controls (rev.~10) bound what the
constraint and the clean training cost: an unconstrained \texttt{resnet18}
at the same budget, and the same CNN retrained with the rule-reader's
degradation augmentation (Sec.~\ref{sec:frontier}).

\emph{Statistical protocol.} Results are reported per target (never only as a
four-target median), with the held-out \emph{rule} as the unit of resampling.
Paired differences carry rule-level cluster-bootstrap intervals ($10^4$
resamples); each method pair is classified per target as \emph{superiority}
(median $\Delta R^2\ge0.10$, $95\%$ CI excluding $0$), \emph{practical
equivalence} (TOST: $90\%$ CI within $(-\delta,+\delta)$, $\delta=0.05$,
reported also at $\delta=0.02$ and $0.10$), or \emph{inconclusive}---the three
labels are not interchangeable and we use only the one earned. Split
uncertainty beyond the registered split is quantified for the deterministic
methods by leave-one-orbit-out over all $88$ ECA orbits and $20$ repeated outer
splits on radius two; the CNN is reported at the registered split with its
five-seed spread, and retraining across splits was not performed. A
cross-fitted stacking test measures incremental \rtwo{} (margin
$0.02$, CI excluding $0$) in both directions between the network and the five
statistics.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Rule reconstruction is simulation-limited}
\label{sec:results-mech}

\emph{The matched-regime verdict is an identity, and we state it as one.}
Write the cached target as $Y_1=\theta+\varepsilon_1$ and a second independent
replicate of it as $Y_2=\theta+\varepsilon_2$. Conditional on exact table
recovery, the mechanistic estimator re-runs the target's own measurement
procedure on an independent stream at the same $n_{\mathrm{pairs}}$, so it
returns $\hat Y=\theta+\varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon$ distributed as
$\varepsilon_1$ and independent of it. Then
\begin{equation}
(\hat Y,\,Y_1)\ \stackrel{d}{=}\ (Y_2,\,Y_1),
\label{eq:exchangeable}
\end{equation}
so $R^2(\hat Y,Y_1)$ and the independent-replicate benchmark are the
\emph{same random variable}: there is no hypothesis to test, only an
implementation to audit. What is genuinely empirical in the matched regime is
therefore the \emph{reconstruction rate} and what the failures cost, not the
agreement.

That rate is high but not unity. The estimator recovers the rule exactly for
$100\%$ of ECA and $97.5\%$ of radius-two held-out rules, and the resulting
predictions sit at the replicate benchmark as Eq.~\eqref{eq:exchangeable}
requires: on ECA, survival $0.985$ against a benchmark of $0.987$ (CI
$[0.978,0.992]$); on radius two, survival $0.977$ against $0.974$
$[0.945,0.984]$ and cone fill $0.985$ against $0.959$ $[0.900,0.987]$. A
paired rule-bootstrap equivalence analysis confirms the arithmetic on six of
eight target--panel comparisons and is resolution-limited on the other two;
because it tests our implementation---stream independence, exactness of
recovery, unbiased scoring---rather than a scientific question, it is reported
in full as a correctness audit in Appendix~\ref{app:repro}. Scored
against the $16\times$-larger reference simulation instead of the noisy cache,
the same predictions rise to the latent ICC ceiling (survival: ECA $0.994$
against ICC $0.994$; radius two $0.988$ against $0.987$)---the residual was
the comparison target's noise, not estimator error. The
direct estimators do not share that rise: rescored against the same reference, the CNN's radius-two median moves by $-0.000$ (from
$0.859$) and the boosted baseline's by $+0.007$ (from $0.845$)---so their
shortfall is genuine estimator error; the $1-\mathrm{ICC}$ headroom of a
perfect latent-mean predictor (Sec.~\ref{sec:reliability}) is nowhere in
play for the models we trained.

Table~\ref{tab:rho} restates this in replicate-noise units, which changes how
large the gap looks. The mechanistic estimator sits at
$\rho=1.0$--$1.8$, its CI covering $\sqrt2$ on every target and panel; the
direct estimators sit at $\rho=3$--$33$. Two further readings follow.
\emph{First}, \rtwo{} flatters the direct estimators most where the target is
most reliable: the CNN's ECA spreading rate reads $0.956$, which sounds like a
near miss, and $\rho=27$, which is not. \emph{Second}, on the one target where
\rtwo{} suggests real skill---damage survival---most of that skill is mode
assignment. Restricting to rules of \emph{intermediate} survival (in
$[0.1,0.9]$: $14/18$ ECA orbits, $40/160$ radius-two rules), the mechanistic
\rtwo{} holds at $0.90$ (ECA) and $0.94$ (radius two), while the boosted
baseline and the CNN fall to $-0.87$ and $-1.25$ on ECA and to $0.41$ and
$0.66$ on radius two---worse than predicting the band mean on the elementary
panel---even though both call the ordered/chaotic mode correctly for $89$--$100\%$
of rules. The direct estimators are, on this target, mode classifiers that
have been read as regressors.

The ``global'' radius-two panel is deliberately \emph{enriched}: all $57$
\emph{signature-complex} rules---a criterion label meaning the rule meets
the registered damage-signature thresholds of Fig.~\ref{fig:landscape}, not
a validated behavioural class---are force-held (never trained on), so they
form $35.6\%$ of the $160$-rule panel against $7.1\%$ of the $800$-rule
sampling universe. We disclose that signature membership was computed by the
registered criterion-9 thresholds from the same seed-0 target cache that
supplies the evaluation labels. Decomposing the panel
shows the enrichment is \emph{conservative} for the headline claim: on the
$103$ stratified-random rules alone the mechanistic median is $0.995$
(signature subpanel $0.982$), and post-stratifying the pooled estimate to the
universe prevalence gives $0.994$---while the seed-0 CNN moves the other way,
from $0.859$ enriched to $0.821$ random and $0.830$ post-stratified.
Reweighting to the representative distribution \emph{widens} the gap between
the families.

Force-holding has a second effect that reweighting cannot undo: the
training split then contains \emph{no} signature-complex rules, where a
representative one would hold ${\sim}7\%$. The mechanistic estimator is
invariant to that by construction and the learned ones are not, so the
comparison is in principle handicapped. The measured direction is the
opposite---the seed-0 CNN scores \emph{higher} on the complex subpanel
($0.832$) than on the random one ($0.821$)---but that is an observation, not a
control, so we ran one (Sec.~\ref{sec:frontier}(iv)); it puts the complex rules
back into training and moves the CNN \emph{down}.

\subsection{No stable ordering between the direct estimators}
\label{sec:paired}

The paired comparisons give the network and the
five-statistic baseline no global ranking. On radius two ($160$ held-out rules,
adequately powered) the CNN is ahead on damage survival ($\Delta R^2=+0.08$,
$95\%$ CI $[+0.002,+0.201]$ excluding $0$ but below the $0.10$ superiority
margin), the baseline is \emph{superior} on cone fill ($\Delta R^2=-0.21$, CI
$[-0.38,-0.07]$), and fraction and rate are \emph{inconclusive} at
$\delta=0.05$ (equivalent only at the loose $\delta=0.10$; fraction's CI
$[-0.09,-0.00]$ weakly favours the baseline). On ECA ($18$ held-out orbits)
every CNN--baseline contrast is inconclusive except spreading rate (equivalent
at $\delta=0.05$); that panel is underpowered for tight per-target intervals
and we do not read a ranking into it. The mechanistic estimator,
by contrast, meets the registered superiority rule against the baseline on
survival, rate and cone fill ($\Delta R^2$ up to $+0.39$) and against the CNN
on fraction, rate and cone fill (up to $+0.60$); the two remaining contrasts
(fraction vs.\ the baseline, survival vs.\ the CNN) have intervals excluding
zero in its favour with point differences just below the margin. No comparison
on any target in either space favours a direct method over it.

\subsection{What the network adds: complementarity, not redundancy}
\label{sec:stacking}

The stacking test asks whether one method's prediction
explains held-out residual variance beyond another's. It is a
\emph{cross-fitted diagnostic computed within the held-out panel}, licensing
a complementarity conclusion and not a deployment one: each row is one
held-out rule, the base is a repeated-$K$-fold out-of-fold ridge on the five
statistics with standardization refit inside every fold, and the CNN
predictions come from the already-trained network without any use of
held-out labels. The base is therefore \emph{not} the boosted baseline of
record (survival $0.56$ versus $0.81$, cone fill $0.14$ versus $0.60$). The
answer is target-specific: adding the CNN to the five statistics yields a
large, significant increment on damage survival ($+0.32$, CI
$[+0.23,+0.48]$, base $0.56\to0.88$), small significant increments on
fraction and rate, and none on cone fill; the reverse direction adds
nothing. With the boosted baseline of record as base the survival increment
remains significant but smaller ($+0.08$, CI
$[+0.02,+0.18]$, $0.80\to0.88$): the $+0.32$ headline is base-specific; the
base-independent statement is that the network carries survival information
both bases miss. Whether that information is \emph{harvestable} has a
measured answer: a deployment-style stack (the same
regressors, ridge fit only on training rules, applied once to the untouched
panel) reaches an overall median of $0.88$ but realizes essentially none of
the survival increment ($+0.001$ over the CNN alone, n.s.; fraction $+0.02$
and rate $+0.03$ do transfer). Only the within-panel diagnostic detects the
survival signal: the network \emph{carries} information that texture
statistics miss---exactly where both direct methods sit farthest below the
reliability benchmark---but a practitioner cannot yet buy it with a linear
meta-model.

\emph{How much of the direct estimators' score is generalisation?} A
shortcut-learning framing needs a baseline that separates ``recognises which
training rule this resembles'' from ``generalises to an unseen table'', so we
built the cheapest possible one: for each held-out diagram, return the
\emph{cached target of the nearest training rule}---no training, no
simulation, a lookup. In the five-statistic space it reaches median held-out
\rtwo{} $0.821$ (radius two) and $0.828$ (ECA); in the CNN's own $64$-d
bottleneck, $0.862$ and $0.881$. Those are not below the trained estimators,
they are level with them: the radius-two CNN scores $0.864$ and the boosted
baseline $0.845$, and on ECA retrieval in the bottleneck ($0.881$)
\emph{exceeds} the network's own regression head ($0.852$). Under the
registered reading, this is evidence that the direct estimators' accuracy on
this benchmark is substantially rule recognition rather than extrapolation to
unseen tables---consistent with the identity probes of
Sec.~\ref{sec:probes}, and a caution for any single-diagram behaviour
benchmark whose rule space is densely sampled. It does not touch the
mechanistic estimator, which never consults a training rule. The mechanistic estimator's increment over the five statistics
remains far larger on every target ($+0.14$ to $+0.84$), so the practical
order of preference under the matched observation model is clear. All
increments carry rule-bootstrap $95\%$ CIs over held-out rules with folds
grouped by rule and every transformation fit inside folds; a percentile
interval of an increment can exceed $1$ when a bootstrap draw's base \rtwo{}
is negative, which is one more reason to read Table~\ref{tab:rho} alongside.

\subsection{Why: the rule is readable, including from the network}
\label{sec:probes}

The mechanism behind these results is that the local rule pervades every
representation of the diagram. The deterministic inverter recovers it exactly
(above); linear probes quantify the same fact for the learned and handcrafted
representations, in two tasks that answer different questions. \emph{Identity probes} (stratified split over diagrams;
train and test diagrams disjoint, rule classes shared---within-rule decoding
across new diagrams; $88$ and $800$ classes, majority baseline $\approx0.011$
and $0.001$) recover the rule from the CNN's own $64$-d bottleneck at balanced
accuracy $0.88$ (ECA) and $0.95$ (radius two), versus $0.65$/$0.30$ from the
five statistics. \emph{Truth-table-bit probes} (leave-rules-out GroupKFold;
test rules never seen by the probe---out-of-rule transfer of the reading
mechanism; chance $0.5$) reach $0.78$/$0.61$ from the bottleneck versus
$0.69$/$0.55$ from the statistics. The $2\times2$ first layer therefore does
\emph{not} prevent rule recovery; we retired that claim. What the probes
establish is accessibility, not use: they show the information survives into
the bottleneck, and the mechanistic estimator shows a rule-first route attains
the reliability benchmark. Whether the network's regression head \emph{causally
relies} on rule information is not established here; rule-scrubbing and
rule-provision interventions that would test it are future work
(Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\subsection{Estimators for the degraded regime: the frontier race}
\label{sec:frontier}

The natural repairs are a rule-\emph{posterior} simulator in the
intermediate regime and a learned rule-reader beyond it, and we benchmark
both [registered numbers-free as rev.~9 before measurement; per-estimator
status in Fig.~\ref{fig:frontier}].

\emph{(i) The pseudo-posterior decoder}---the probabilistic move of the
identification programme~\cite{adamatzky1994}, and the name we use for it
throughout. The model is
an \emph{entrywise pseudo-posterior}: each observed transition count enters
an independent per-entry Binomial likelihood in which a bit flip corrupts
only the \emph{output} cell at rate $\varepsilon$ (supplied, or estimated
from observation self-consistency over a fixed ten-point grid), under an
independent Bernoulli prior per entry at the observed marginal
one-frequency. Input-side corruption---which mis-bins transitions and
correlates overlapping neighbourhoods---is deliberately \emph{not} modelled;
it is absorbed into the effective $\varepsilon$, and the calibration
measurement below prices that approximation empirically. Absorption into
$\varepsilon$ addresses \emph{bias} but not \emph{count inflation}: every cell
is the output of one transition and an input of $2r+1$ others, and
neighbourhoods one step apart share $2r$ of their $2r+1$ input bits, so the
$\mathrm{total}_k\approx500$ transitions per entry carry an effective sample
size well below their nominal count. The log-odds are correspondingly
overconfident---the worked example in Appendix~\ref{app:algorithms} reaches
$\ell\approx77$, a certainty of $1-10^{-33}$---which is the mechanism behind
the calibration collapse measured below. We built neither the cheap repair (dividing
counts by an overlap factor, or fitting an overdispersion parameter) nor the
correct one (marginalizing the latent clean diagram), so the noise-band
verdicts below bound \emph{the estimators evaluated}, not the
read-then-simulate family. Simulating tables sampled from it \emph{restores
identification across the intermediate band}: at $40\%$ masking its median
\rtwo{} is $0.85$ against the deterministic inverter's $0.24$; at $50\%$,
$0.59$ against $-0.77$; at IC density $0.1$, $0.77$ against $0.08$; and it is
exactly polarity-invariant where the frozen CNN degrades under label flips
($0.99$ vs $0.59$). Estimating $\varepsilon$ by self-consistency matches the
supplied-$\varepsilon$ variant throughout. On the noise axis the advantage is
real but bounded: best of all methods to ${\sim}3\%$ ($0.55$ vs $0.38$ at
$2\%$), collapsing beyond ${\sim}5\%$. Its nominal $1\sigma$ intervals tell the
same story: empirical coverage holds at or above ${\sim}68\%$ across the
masking band where the decoder is recommended ($0.86$ clean to $0.74$ at
$50\%$) but collapses along the noise axis ($0.58$ at $2\%$, $0.13$ at
$20\%$)---beyond its range the intervals are not merely wide but
overconfident. That
noise-axis overconfidence is exactly the effective-sample-size price above:
masking removes transitions cleanly, so the independence approximation stays
benign, whereas bit flips corrupt inputs as well as outputs. Two further
approximations are worth flagging. The self-consistency statistic $D$
(Appendix~\ref{app:algorithms}) estimates a minority fraction and is therefore
downward-biased at small counts---identically zero for an entry seen once---so
$\hat\varepsilon$ is a lower bound precisely on the sparsely exercised entries
where the posterior matters most. And the same $D$ does a second job in radius
selection, where it is inflated by aliasing rather than by noise: the
smallest-consistent rule costs ${\sim}0.12$ median \rtwo{} on clean diagrams
and degrades under noise (neighbourhood detection is itself a studied
identification subproblem~\cite{yangbillings2000}). Its $0.005$ tolerance is
a convention, not a criterion; because $D$ is non-increasing in $r$ under
nesting, the principled replacement is the marginal likelihood of the
entrywise Beta--Bernoulli model already in hand, penalized by the $2^{2r+1}$
parameter count.

\emph{(ii) Learned rule-reader:} a small ($3\times10^4$-parameter) network
trained once, on one seed, on training-split rules to predict table bits from
degradation-augmented diagrams, feeding the exact simulator either its
thresholded (MAP) table or an average over tables sampled from its per-bit
probabilities. It recovers $0.73$ of held-out table bits and never a full
table exactly, and the MAP variant fails everywhere (median \rtwo{}
$\le-2.2$): a single mostly-right table is a badly wrong dynamical system.
Sampling salvages the signal. The sampled variant is the only one of the eight
registered estimators that does not collapse in the dead zone
(Fig.~\ref{fig:frontier}), holding median \rtwo{} in $[-0.04,0.17]$ from
$7.5$ to $20\%$ noise---essentially flat, its bit accuracy dropping only
$0.73{\to}0.70$---while the inverter falls to $-5.6$ and the frozen CNN to
$-0.93$. Holding \rtwo{}${\approx}0$ is not identification; it moves the
frontier without restoring it.

\emph{(iii) Fairness controls} [registered numbers-free as rev.~10 before
measurement]. The comparison above hands the read-then-simulate family two estimators
adapted for degradation while the direct CNN stays clean-trained and
constrained, so we ran two symmetric controls, each a single seed on the
identical grid cells: the same CNN retrained with the reader's registered
degradation augmentation, and an unconstrained \texttt{resnet18} at the same
budget ($11{,}172{,}292$ parameters, $354\times$ the constrained CNN's
$31{,}524$; clean-trained). On this fixed $80$-rule panel the result reverses one rev-9
conclusion and confirms the other. The noise ``dead zone'' was an
adaptation artifact, not an estimator-family boundary: the seed-0
degradation-trained CNN holds median \rtwo{} $0.51$--$0.62$ from $3\%$
to $15\%$ noise---where no previously tested estimator exceeded
$0.17$---meeting the registered extension rule (bootstrap CI excluding the
sampled reader's point value from below) at every noise cell up to $15\%$;
at $20\%$ that seed and the reader are comparable ($0.17$ vs $0.12$),
neither reliable. It pays a robustness tax on clean diagrams
and does not approach the decoder under masking ($0.48$ vs $0.85$ at
$40\%$) or extreme density ($0.20$ vs $0.77$). Meanwhile the third
registered hypothesis survives the controls: at every cell with per-bit
table recovery ${\ge}0.95$ the best read-then-simulate estimator remains
unbeaten---zero violations---and the unconstrained network is no better on
the grid's clean cells than the $31$k constrained one ($0.55$ vs $0.65$)
while degrading faster under noise ($-0.60$ at $20\%$); on the full
$160$-rule clean protocol it trades cone fill up for survival down at a
slightly lower median, and every mechanistic superiority verdict survives.
The matched-regime
negative result is not an artifact of the anti-shortcut constraint.

\emph{Seed variance was mostly the selection rule} [registered, rev.~13].
The rev-11 seed replication reported a factor-${\sim}2$ spread (band
$0.24$--$0.62$; seed 1 trained to $0.24$--$0.33$) and attributed it to
final-epoch weight selection under an oscillating validation curve. That
diagnosis was testable, so we tested it: the three seeds were retrained with
the final-epoch rule replaced by \emph{selection of the epoch minimising loss
on an inner fold of training-rule diagrams} (never the held-out panel, which
would be selection on the test set), everything else identical, and the noise
axis rescored. The spread largely collapses. The band becomes $0.46$--$0.64$
across all three seeds---seed 1, the outlier, moves to $0.46$--$0.56$---and
the registered stability rule that \emph{failed} at rev 11 is now \emph{met}:
every seed's band-cell median lies inside the seed-0 rule-bootstrap CI at
every cell from $3\%$ to $15\%$. Clean-cell scores rise in step
($0.43$--$0.55$ against $0.29$--$0.51$), so part of the ``robustness tax'' was
also an artifact of stopping at epoch $60$. We therefore no longer report
${\sim}2\times$ run-to-run variance as a property of this estimator family: at
this budget \emph{with} checkpoint selection it is nearer $1.4\times$. What
does not change is the ceiling---at $20\%$ noise the selected checkpoints
reach only $0.18$--$0.36$---or the family-level ordering.

\emph{(iv) Stratified split without force-holding} [registered, rev.~13]. The
canonical panel force-holds all $57$ signature-complex rules, so the training
split contains none of them; the mechanistic estimator is invariant to that
distortion by construction and the learned estimators are not. We retrained the
CNN (three seeds) on a stratified leave-rules-out split with force-holding
disabled---$52$ of the $57$ signature-complex rules now in training, $5$
($3.1\%$) in the held-out panel---and rescored every family on it. The
objection is removed and no verdict moves: mechanistic median \rtwo{} $0.992$,
boosted baseline $0.780$, CNN $0.778$ ($0.74$--$0.78$ across seeds), against
$0.991$, $0.845$ and $0.859$ on the enriched panel. The registered rule
downgrades the family-gap claim to the stratified number if any target's gap
shrinks by more than $0.10$ median \rtwo{}; the largest shrink over both direct
estimators is $0.07$ (the boosted baseline's cone fill), and two of the
network's gaps \emph{widen}---survival by $0.16$ and cone fill by $0.34$.
Representative training does not close the gap. One counter-observation, resting on
five rules and correspondingly weak: the CNN still scores $-0.81$ on the five
held-out complex rules against $0.78$ on the rest, where on the enriched panel
it had scored \emph{higher} on complex rules than on random ones.

\emph{Panel replication.} Because the frontier rests on a fixed $80$-rule
subsample, we re-simulated the entire noise axis on the \emph{complementary}
$80$ held-out rules at identical budgets. The family structure replicates:
read-then-simulate owns the low-noise cells, the degradation-trained network
is strongest or statistically tied from $3\%$, and at $20\%$ no estimator is
reliable. No cell winner is re-stated under the registered rule. Two level
shifts measure panel sensitivity in its own right: the selected-checkpoint
band reads $0.39$--$0.81$ on the complement against $0.46$--$0.64$ on the
canonical panel, and the pseudo-posterior's collapse point moves---it holds
$0.79/0.70/0.61$ at $3/5/7.5\%$ on the complement where it had collapsed by

beat the best read-then-simulate estimator (Appendix~\ref{app:repro} records

\subsection{Cost: accuracy versus compute}
\label{sec:compute}

This subsection and the next are \emph{secondary} analyses; every claim of
the paper rests on Secs.~\ref{sec:results-mech}--\ref{sec:frontier}. The
mechanistic estimator pays per query (rule inference plus a fresh
simulation); the network pays once. In a deployment vignette on one
machine---both methods timed on the \emph{same} single CPU core,
warm-started, per diagram---the mechanistic estimator
costs $283$\,ms per diagram at the production budget ($256$ pairs;
embarrassingly parallel over diagrams), against $1.0$\,ms for a CNN forward
pass on that thread ($2.0$\,ms batched at $256$; $2.2$\,ms on the Metal GPU,
where dispatch overhead dominates a $31$k-parameter model) and $1.4$\,ms for
the five statistics; one-time costs ($\approx22$\,min per CNN seed versus
$15$\,s for the boosted baseline) give a break-even of $\approx4700$
queries. That $283$\,ms is a first-party single-core Python figure, not an
algorithmic property: a $127$-cell ring fits in two \texttt{uint64} words, so a
bit-packed implementation evolves $64$ twin runs per word and would plausibly
cut two orders of magnitude, moving break-even toward $\approx50$ queries---the
unmeasured number only strengthens the conclusion below. The
accuracy-versus-budget curve is one-sided: even at a
$16$-pair budget ($17$\,ms per diagram) the mechanistic median \rtwo{} is
$0.924$, above both direct estimators at any cost, and $32$ pairs ($32$\,ms)
give $0.966$. Under the matched observation model the mechanistic estimator
is the most accurate at every simulation budget we measured, so the
network's case must rest on degraded-observation regimes
(Fig.~\ref{fig:ident}) or extreme-throughput screening, not on accuracy.

The curve's zero-budget end is anchored by an analytic member [exploratory,
rev.~12]: the annealed approximation~\cite{derridapomeau1986} applied to the
reconstructed table. At $2.7$\,ms per diagram it fails on every target (median
held-out \rtwo{} $-2.15$) and its survival criterion never beats the panel's
$98\%$ always-survives base rate. This is a weak test of a strong claim---an asymptotic
mean-field approximation scored against finite-horizon conditional statistics
on a $127$-ring is expected to fail---but it locates the failure: the targets
measure exactly the quenched correlation annealing discards (an additive rule's
difference pattern is a deterministic gasket, cone density $0.26$, not a mixed
density near $0.5$), so the mean-field tier of the rule-table cost
hierarchy~\cite{vispoel2026structure} does not transfer and simulation is the
cheapest usable tier we measure. In the setting the approximation was built
for---random Boolean networks with redrawn connections---annealed predictions
track the quenched overlap dynamics closely~\cite{derridaweisbuch1986}; a
single fixed rule is the opposite limit.

\subsection{Descriptive by-products, with independent ground-truth evaluation}
\label{sec:constructive}

\emph{Phenotype maps for non-uniform CA.} Sliding the pretrained
five-statistic regressor over a composed (two-rule) diagram yields a
spatially resolved phenotype map with no additional training at deployment, in
the spirit of the local statistical-complexity filters of computational
mechanics~\cite{shalizi2006} but predicting a perturbation-defined quantity.
Scored against an independent location-resolved ground truth, its advantage is
\emph{inconclusive} under the registered framework (mean Spearman $0.67$ vs
$0.61$, $\Delta=+0.047$, $95\%$ CI $[-0.002,+0.102]$): a small, statistically
unresolved edge. Design and full numbers are in the released repository.

\emph{A damage-signature landscape of the radius-two space.} The same
statistics over $3000$ uniformly sampled canonical rules give
Fig.~\ref{fig:landscape}: $7.8\%$ (Wilson $95\%$ CI $[6.9,8.8]\%$) of rules
satisfy the registered damage-signature criterion (persistent,
sub-ballistic, sparse-cone damage), drawn in damage coordinates in the spirit
of Li and Packard's map of the elementary rule space~\cite{lipackard1990}.
This is the prevalence of a \emph{finite-horizon criterion} under the stated
sampling distribution, not a validated glider census---and the
\emph{finite-horizon} qualifier does real work, which we quantify because an
earlier draft did not. The horizon $\lfloor w/2r\rfloor-1$ is $62$ for ECA and
$30$ for radius two at $w=127$, so comparing prevalences across the two spaces
compares different experiments; damage survival is monotone decreasing in
horizon, so the longer elementary horizon kills more marginal rules. Rerunning
the elementary criterion at the \emph{matched} $T=30$ raises its prevalence
from $3/88$ ($3.4\%$, the set $\{54,106,110\}$) to $6/88$ ($6.8\%$,
adding $37$, $45$ and $60$). At matched horizon the cross-space gap therefore
essentially closes ($6.8\%$ vs $7.8\%$), and the criterion's precision against
literature class IV falls from $2/3$ to $1/3$. We accordingly do not claim a
richer complex region at radius two; the honest statement is that this
criterion's prevalence is horizon-sensitive and that the two spaces look alike
once the horizons are matched. On ECA, where literature ground
truth exists, the signature selects $\{54,106,110\}$: both canonical
class-IV rules (sensitivity $1.0$) with one false positive, rule $106$,
which the classical schemes list as chaotic~\cite{wolfram1984,lipackard1990}
but whose damage signature sits between the chaotic and complex clusters in
our measurements (precision $2/3$, balanced accuracy $0.99$). We attempted an
independent localized-seed check and report it as a null: the detector
performs at or below chance on radius two (balanced accuracy $0.39$), so it
validates nothing either way (Appendix~\ref{app:priorwork}). We therefore do
not promote the $7.8\%$ region to ``complex'' or ``glider-supporting''---that
would require long-horizon curated validation we have not done.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Under a fully observed, noiseless, deterministic, synchronous, binary
observation model with known radius, a single spacetime diagram identified
the local rule exactly for every elementary and $97.5\%$ of radius-two
held-out rules. Everything else in the matched regime follows: reconstructing
the generator and re-running the stipulated experiment is \emph{exchangeable}
with re-measuring the target, so the agreement is arithmetic, not evidence
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:exchangeable}). The claim worth making is conditional: \emph{a nominally
single-observation amortization benchmark is solved by system identification}
whenever the observation determines the generator within a known or searchable
model class, the inversion is tractable, and the target's own measurement
procedure can be re-run at matched budget. Benchmark designers who
intend to test representation learning must break that encoding (noise,
partial observation, unknown model class) or accept that they are benchmarking
identification.

We should be plain about how far that travels. We do not name a published
benchmark this invalidates: we do not know of one posed on a system whose
observations encode the generator as completely as a CA diagram does, and we
have not surveyed for it. What this paper offers is a controlled instance in
which the identification baseline can be built exactly and calibrated, plus a
frontier that says where it stops working---and two measurements suggesting
the failure mode is not exotic. Both direct estimators' survival ``skill'' is
mode assignment (Sec.~\ref{sec:results-mech}), and a training-set lookup
matches the network in its own embedding (Sec.~\ref{sec:stacking}); neither
diagnostic is CA-specific, and both are cheap to run on any benchmark whose
generator space is densely sampled.

The lesson connects literatures that do not currently cite one another. In
continuous-state settings the identification route is equation
discovery~\cite{brunton2016,schmidtlipson2009}: once the generator is
recovered, every simulation-defined functional of it---Lyapunov exponents
included---follows by re-running, so a damage-response benchmark posed there
would face the same baseline. Simulation-based inference names the trade we
quantify~\cite{cranmer2020}, and the identifiability frontier is a worked
example of choosing between an amortized predictor and per-observation
inference on observation quality. At field scale, McGreivy and Hakim document
that most machine-learning-for-PDE papers claiming to beat standard numerics
compared against weak baselines~\cite{mcgreivy2024}; this is the controlled,
closed-world version of that critique, with the strong baseline constructed
explicitly and calibrated against replicate reliability.

The neural results are deliberately scoped. For \emph{this} constrained
architecture at \emph{this} training budget, direct regression neither reaches
the reliability benchmark nor dominates five cheap statistics, and the two
trade places by target; an unconstrained architecture $354\times$ larger at
the same budget changes no verdict, so this is not the constraint's doing. The
network's one demonstrated edge---incremental survival information, $+0.32$
over the five-statistic base and $+0.08$ over the boosted baseline of
record---survives cross-fitting, but sits next to two findings that bound how
much of its score is generalisation at all: within the intermediate survival band it does not
outperform the band mean (Sec.~\ref{sec:results-mech}), and a training-set
lookup in its own bottleneck reproduces its median \rtwo{}
(Sec.~\ref{sec:stacking}). We do not conclude that deep networks cannot close
the gap: the probes show rule information is accessible, and whether
exploiting it is an architecture, objective, or optimization question is open.
The rev-9 reader and the rev-10 degradation-trained control sharpen
it---beyond ${\sim}3$--$7.5\%$ noise the strongest estimator is the direct
network trained for the corruption, the sampled reader merely holds
\rtwo{}${\approx}0$, and neither approaches simulation-limited accuracy, while
under masking and extreme densities the pseudo-posterior decoder extends the
mechanistic route instead. Whether a larger reader, a differentiable simulator
in the loop, joint reader--simulator training, or corruption-trained networks
at scale close that gap is a concrete open problem, next to rule-scrubbing and
rule-provision interventions to test causality. One
omission we regard as the most consequential: the correctly specified decoder
for bit-flip observation noise marginalizes the latent clean diagram---a
factor graph with known structure, amenable to Gibbs sampling or
expectation--maximization on a $127\times127$ binary lattice---and we did not
build it. In a paper arguing that baselines must be constructed carefully
before conclusions are drawn about them, that is the uncomfortable gap, and it
is why the noise-axis verdicts are scoped to the estimators evaluated.

How far does the frontier travel beyond binary deterministic CA? Each axis has
a named analogue: observation noise to measurement noise in equation
discovery, where recovery is likewise noise-brittle~\cite{brunton2016};
masking to missing sensors; initial-condition density to insufficient
excitation---our under-exercised rule tables are the finite-alphabet form of
the persistency-of-excitation condition of classical system identification;
label flips to symmetry freedoms, under which equivariant estimators are
invariant by construction; and known radius to known model order, whose
relaxation costs ${\sim}0.12$ median \rtwo{} here. Each \emph{collapse} is
measured on CA; every \emph{transfer} is conjectured, as is the repair
ordering (exact, then pseudo-posterior, then corruption-trained). A stochastic
generator, by contrast, changes the problem in kind, and is where we expect
this paper's thesis to stop being a near-identity. If the generator is a table
of probabilities $\pi_k=\Pr[s_{t+1,i}{=}1\mid k]$ rather than bits, a single
diagram gives only a \emph{noisy} estimate of $\pi$, so read-then-simulate
acquires genuine estimation error trading off against a direct estimator's,
and the question becomes \emph{where along the determinism axis} the
identification baseline stops dominating. Our machinery carries over: the
entrywise pseudo-posterior of Sec.~\ref{sec:frontier}(i) is already a
Beta--Bernoulli posterior over $\pi_k$, natively the right estimator there
with the $\varepsilon$ apparatus dropping out, and its independence
approximation becomes much milder, since outputs really are conditionally
independent given inputs and only input-side window overlap remains. The obvious testbed is the stochastic
cellular automaton of Domany and Kinzel~\cite{domanykinzel1984}: three
conditional probabilities, deterministic rules exactly at the corners of that
cube, and one face equivalent to generalized directed percolation, so the
determinism axis is also a line through a known critical phenomenon. We have not
run it, so nothing here licenses conclusions for that case---but
it is the experiment that would turn a corollary of
Richards \emph{et al.}~\cite{richards1990} into a result.

Every claim is bounded by the protocol tuple (Bernoulli$(1/2)$ ICs, ring
$127$, radius-aware horizon): the statistics are finite-horizon and
measure-dependent by construction~\cite{gilman1987,culikyu1988}, and
Fig.~\ref{fig:ident}(d) exhibits that dependence directly. Within that scope the guidance is
plain and carries its price tag (Table~\ref{tab:guidance},
Sec.~\ref{sec:compute}): match the observation model and read the rule; under
moderate corruption read it \emph{probabilistically}; beyond that, train for
the corruption you expect; and past $20\%$ noise or $50\%$ masking recommend
nothing we tested. For the broader program of behaviour-first (phenotype)
analysis of CA~\cite{wolfram2002,lipackard1990,israeli2006}, the
damage-signature landscape stands as a usable descriptive artefact, described
at the strength its independent evaluation supports and no
stronger~\cite{kleinberg2002,mitchell1993}.

\section{Reproducibility and registration status}
\label{app:repro}

All experiments run on one Apple-silicon machine (Metal/CPU, fp32); the
pipeline is first-party except PyTorch and scikit-learn. Targets, splits and
panels are keyed on fixed seeds; the mechanistic estimator and reliability
replicates use random streams provably independent of the target cache (prime
seed offsets on per-rule \texttt{SeedSequence}s), so agreement between them is
never contamination. All decision rules, margins and panels were committed to
a timestamped private repository before the corresponding measurements; the
full history is released with the article. Because an in-review repository is
not independently auditable, we label every analysis here rather than lean on
the word ``pre-registered''.

\emph{Registered} (rule committed numbers-free before measurement): the
damage-signature criterion; holdout split; paired inference and TOST margins;
stacking margin; reliability design; mechanistic estimator and completion
policy; probe design; detector- and map-validation designs; the frontier grid
design, estimator definitions, verdict rules and three hypotheses (rev.~9);
the two fairness controls (rev.~10); and the four rev.-13 measurements
answering the fourth review---checkpoint selection (M9), the stratified-split
control (M10), the retrieval baseline (M11) and the horizon-matched signature
(M12).
\emph{Post-hoc confirmatory} (added at revision, on data or rules not used to
tune it): the replicate-agreement correction; the large-simulation reference;
confusion-matrix reporting; TOST margin sensitivity; the pseudo-posterior on
the pre-existing axes; the headline-base stacking variant (rev.~10); the seven
rev.-11 review-response analyses (reference rescoring, paired equivalence,
panel decomposition, per-diagram audit, seed replication, deployment-style
stack, complement-panel replication); and the rev.-13 re-expressions of
released measurements ($\rho$, per-panel reliability, target dependence,
reduced-budget ceilings, survival-band stratification).
\emph{Exploratory}: the identifiability phase diagram; the accuracy--compute
curve with its annealed zero-budget member (rev.~12); the learned rule-reader;
and the label-noise and unknown-radius axes.

The three frontier hypotheses (rev.~9) were registered verbatim before
measurement; their earned verdicts---\emph{confirmed}; \emph{split}, weakly
confirmed on noise and refuted under masking; and \emph{confirmed with zero
violations}---are stated in Sec.~\ref{sec:frontier}, as is the one reporting
item outstanding from rev.~9, the calibration of the posterior's nominal
$1\sigma$ predictive intervals, completed here.

\emph{Correctness audit of the matched regime.} Equation~\eqref{eq:exchangeable}
makes the matched-regime agreement an identity, so the paired equivalence
analysis registered at rev.~11 is an audit of the implementation, not a test
of a hypothesis: it can only fail if the estimator's random stream is not
independent of the target cache, if reconstruction is not exact where claimed,
or if the scoring is biased. The same rule-bootstrap resamples are pushed
through $R^2_{\mathrm{mech}}$ and $R^2_{\mathrm{replicate}}$, giving a CI for
their difference against a tolerance of $\max(0.01,\,1-\mathrm{ICC}_t)$. This
tolerance is a convention, not an inferential margin---ICC(1) under
heteroscedastic $\sigma_e^2(\text{rule})$ is not the attenuation ceiling of any
particular rule, which is why Sec.~\ref{sec:reliability} declines to lean on
the Gaussian identity and why the audit is not reported as an equivalence
verdict in the main text. Six of eight target--panel comparisons pass: on
radius two, survival differs by $+0.001$ (CI $[-0.008,+0.009]$, tolerance
$\pm0.013$), with fraction and rate tighter still; on ECA, fraction, rate and
cone fill pass. The two that do not are resolution-limited rather than
discrepant---ECA survival's CI ($\pm0.015$ over $18$ rules) exceeds its
$\pm0.010$ tolerance at a point difference of $+0.0003$, and radius-two cone
fill ($+0.006$, CI $[-0.009,+0.022]$, tolerance $\pm0.020$) overshoots only on
the side where the mechanistic estimator beats the replicate. With two of
eight underpowered comparisons, both failing in the favourable direction, we
read this as an audit passed, not as evidence of an asymmetry.

One deviation from the original registration is
disclosed: an early implementation seeded target streams by panel position
rather than rule identity (cross-panel drift up to $0.05$--$0.08$ on
borderline-survival rules); it was replaced by identity keying and the drift
verified to vanish.

\section{Methods supplement}
\label{app:methods}

\emph{Data.} ECA: all $88$ orbit representatives, $256$ diagrams per rule;
radius two: $800$ canonical rules ($857$ drawn, deduplicated by the order-four
reflection/complement orbit), $64$ diagrams per rule; all diagrams
$127\times127$, Bernoulli$(1/2)$ ICs, no transient discarded. \emph{Sampling
measure:} radius-two rules are drawn uniformly over the $2^{32}$ raw
encodings, folded to canonical orbit representatives, and deduplicated;
orbits therefore enter with probability proportional to orbit size (the
natural random-rule measure), \emph{not} uniformly over orbits, and every
prevalence in this paper (including Fig.~\ref{fig:landscape}'s $7.8\%$) is
under this measure. Observed
diagrams and target-estimation streams are disjoint by construction (dataset
seed vs.\ target seed), and no random numbers are shared across methods.
Splits: leave-rules-out, holdout fraction $0.2$, stratified by Wolfram label
where available, seed $0$; radius two force-holds the $57$ signature-complex
panel rules (so the complex subset is never trained on); ECA force-holds rule
$110$ and force-trains rule $54$. Hyperparameters were tuned only on training
rules.

\emph{Evaluation protocol (the unit behind every number).} (1)~A test
instance is a (rule, diagram) pair. (2)~The direct estimators predict per
diagram and are \emph{averaged within rule} before scoring; the mechanistic
and probabilistic decoders use the rule's \emph{first} diagram only, with
cross-diagram stability audited separately---an asymmetry that favours the
direct estimators. (3)~Every reported \rtwo{} is computed at the
rule level: unfitted $1-\mathrm{SS}_{\mathrm{res}}/\mathrm{SS}_{\mathrm{tot}}$
per target over held-out rules, with the held-out panel mean in the
denominator and no refitting; a constant target column yields NaN and is
excluded from medians; negative values are reported as computed. Frontier
bootstrap draws alone are clipped to $[-1,1]$ before taking the median CI:
this binds only in the collapsed cells (noise ${\ge}7.5\%$ and masking
${\ge}50\%$, where point medians reach $-5.6$), it narrows those intervals,
and no verdict in Table~\ref{tab:guidance} rests on a clipped cell. Reporting
$\rho$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:rho}) avoids the issue entirely, since $\rho\ge0$ has no
sign pathology. (4)~The
four-target ``median'' is the median of the four per-target values.
(5)~Bootstrap resampling always draws \emph{rules} with replacement ($10^4$
draws); diagrams are already collapsed into their rule's prediction, so the
diagram-within-rule structure is preserved by construction. (6)~Coverage and
exact-reconstruction rates in the main text are per rule (first diagram);
the per-diagram audit [rev.~11] reports the other unit.

\emph{Targets.} With flip position $x_0$ uniform on the ring, horizon
$T=\lfloor w/(2r)\rfloor-1$ (capped so a single flip's light cone cannot wrap
the periodic boundary), and $d\in\{0,1\}^w$ the cellwise disagreement (the
\emph{difference pattern}) of the twin runs at time $T$:
survival $=\Pr[\exists i: d_i=1]$; fraction $=\mathbb{E}[\bar d\mid\text{surv}]$;
rate $=\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{ext}(d)/(2rT)\mid\text{surv}]$; fill
$=\mathbb{E}[\sum_i d_i/\mathrm{ext}(d)\mid\text{surv}]$. The extent is
computed in flip-centred ring coordinates
$p_i=(i-x_0+\lfloor w/2\rfloor)\bmod w$ over damaged cells as
$\mathrm{ext}(d)=\max_i p_i-\min_i p_i+1$; a single damaged cell has
$\mathrm{ext}=1$ and no wrap can occur under the horizon cap. Conditioning on
survival makes $d$ nonempty, so both conditional denominators are strictly
positive. Two consequences of these definitions are worth stating explicitly.
\emph{(i)} Because extent counts endpoints, $\mathrm{ext}\le 2rT+1$ and so
$\text{rate}\le 1+1/(2rT)=1.008$; the maximum observed anywhere in this paper
is $0.816$. \emph{(ii)} The three conditional targets are not algebraically
independent: up to Jensen, $\text{fill}\approx\bigl(w/2rT\bigr)\cdot
\text{fraction}/\text{rate}$, with $w/2rT=1.058$ at $r=2$ and $1.024$ at
$r=1$. We registered the reading that an empirical $R^2>0.95$ of fill
against that ratio would make cone fill a derived coordinate. It does not:
the measured value is $0.64$ over the $3000$-rule landscape sample and $0.70$
over the $160$-rule held-out panel (Pearson $r=0.86$). The ratio-of-expectations
approximation is therefore informative but loose, and cone fill carries
independent information---consistent with its being the target on which the
five statistics and the CNN differ most (Table~\ref{tab:r2}). Conditional targets
are $0$ when no pair survives (itself the ordered-rule signature); surviving
trial counts therefore vary by rule, one reason survival-adjacent targets are
heteroscedastic and all reliability intervals are bootstrapped.

\emph{Network.} Input $1\times127\times127$; four stages of [Conv $2\times2$,
stride 1, no padding $\to$ BatchNorm $\to$ ReLU $\to$ MaxPool $2\times2$] with
channels $16/32/64/64$; $1\times1$ convolution to a $64$-d bottleneck with
BatchNorm and no final nonlinearity; global average pool; linear head to $4$
targets ($31{,}524$ parameters; receptive field $31\times31$ per bottleneck
position before pooling). AdamW, lr $10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-6}$, batch
$128$, $60$ epochs, no early stopping; targets standardized on training rules.
Augmentations: cyclic shift, reflection, complement (the exact
orbit symmetries). Training cost $\approx22$\,min (radius two) and
$\approx10$\,min (ECA) per seed on the reference machine.

\emph{Baselines.} Ridge ($\alpha=1$) and scikit-learn
\texttt{GradientBoostingRegressor} (defaults, one per target) on the five
features standardized on training rules; the boosted model is stronger on both
spaces and is the baseline of record. Split sensitivity: leave-one-orbit-out
over the $88$ ECA orbits gives per-target \rtwo{} $0.73/0.85/0.95/0.93$,
consistent with the registered split; over $20$ repeated radius-two outer
splits the per-target medians (5th--95th percentile) are $0.76$
$(0.56$--$0.84)$, $0.92$ $(0.90$--$0.93)$, $0.90$ $(0.87$--$0.91)$ and $0.68$
$(0.55$--$0.75)$---the noisy targets carry substantial split variance.

\emph{Verdicts at other margins.} At $\delta=0.02$ the single equivalence
verdict (ECA spreading rate, CNN vs.\ baseline) reverts to inconclusive; at
$\delta=0.10$ radius-two fraction and rate additionally become equivalent; no
superiority verdict changes at any margin, so the ``no stable ordering''
conclusion is margin-insensitive.

\emph{Panels.} The $800$-rule estimator panel is a subset of the $3000$-rule
landscape sample (same sampling stream); landscape prevalence is therefore
computed on the superset and estimator results never touch the extra $2200$
rules. The reliability panel subsamples $250$ of the $800$ (fixed seed).

\section{Decoder algorithms, clean and degraded}
\label{app:algorithms}

Every decoder in this paper reduces to the same sufficient statistics: for
neighbourhood index $k\in\{0,\dots,2^{2r+1}-1\}$, the counts
$(\mathrm{ones}_k,\mathrm{total}_k)$ of observed transitions.

\emph{A1: tabulation.} For every $t<T$ and cell $i$ (periodic in space, the
$t{=}0$ row included as an input row), the index is
$k=\sum_{o=-r}^{r} s_{t,i+o}\,2^{\,r-o}$ (leftmost neighbour most
significant) and the recorded output is $s_{t+1,i}$. The deterministic table
is the per-entry \emph{majority}, $\mathrm{bit}_k =
\mathbb{1}[2\,\mathrm{ones}_k > \mathrm{total}_k]$ (ties to $0$); entries
with $\mathrm{total}_k=0$ are unobserved and completed by the registered
default-$0$ policy; an inconsistency flag records whether any observed entry
was non-unanimous (impossible on clean deterministic data).

\emph{A2: degradations.} Bit-flip noise flips every cell of the diagram
independently with probability $p$, including the $t{=}0$ row, before
tabulation. Masking marks cells unobserved; a transition at $(t,i)$ enters
the counts only if its output cell $(t{+}1,i)$ \emph{and} every input cell
within radius $r$ of $(t,i)$ are observed. At each grid cell of
Sec.~\ref{sec:identifiability} the same corrupted diagram is given to every
estimator.

\emph{A3: pseudo-posterior decoding.} Given a flip rate $\varepsilon$
(clamped away from $\{0,\tfrac12\}$) and a per-entry Bernoulli prior $p_0$
(the observed marginal one-frequency; $\tfrac12$ when nothing is observed),
the per-entry posterior log-odds are
\[
\ell_k=\log\frac{p_0}{1-p_0}
+\bigl(2\,\mathrm{ones}_k-\mathrm{total}_k\bigr)\,
\log\frac{1-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon},
\]
\[
P(\mathrm{bit}_k{=}1)=\sigma(\ell_k),
\]
i.e.\ output-cell flips only, entries treated independently. When
$\varepsilon$ is not supplied it is estimated by observation
self-consistency: the disagreement fraction
$D=\sum_k\min(\mathrm{ones}_k,\mathrm{total}_k-\mathrm{ones}_k)/\sum_k
\mathrm{total}_k$ is matched to the nearest value of the fixed grid
$\{0,0.005,0.01,0.02,0.03,0.05,0.075,0.10,0.15,0.20\}$ [registered, rev.~9].
The estimate is the average of the simulated damage response of $8$ tables
sampled from the independent per-entry posteriors; their spread is the
predictive interval whose calibration Sec.~\ref{sec:frontier} reports.

\emph{A4: radius selection.} Candidates $r\in\{1,2,3\}$: compute $D$ at each
candidate and select the \emph{smallest} whose $D$ is within $0.005$ of the
minimum; a selected $r{<}2$ table is embedded exactly, a selected $r{=}3$
table is projected to radius $2$ by count-weighted majority over its middle
five index bits.

\emph{Worked example} (radius $1$, $\varepsilon=0.05$, $p_0=\tfrac12$). A
neighbourhood observed $30$ times mapping $28{\to}1$, $2{\to}0$: A1 restores
$\mathrm{bit}=1$ by majority and sets the inconsistency flag; A3 gives
$\ell=(56-30)\log(0.95/0.05)\approx 77$, certainty. A sparsely exercised
entry seen $3$ times mapping $2{\to}1$, $1{\to}0$ gets $\ell=\log
19\approx2.9$, $P(\mathrm{bit}{=}1)\approx0.95$: sampled tables disagree
exactly on the entries the diagram cannot pin down, which is what makes the
sampled-table average and its interval informative.
